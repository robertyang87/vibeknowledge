# ASKS #1 — Swim distances: does "about 300 km was covered on water" hold?

Researched 2026-11-06. Claim under test (episode 3 closer): roughly 300 km of the
journey was covered on water. Per segment: distance, source, date, short verbatim
excerpt (all quotes kept under ~15 words), and mode (swimming vs drifting).
Distances are only stated where a source states them; where none exists the
segment is marked NOT FOUND with the searches run. Nothing below is estimated.

## (a) English Channel, Oct 2026 — FOUND, two distinct figures

- **Distance actually swum: ~28 km — Bushby's own estimate.** The Times
  (published 2026-10-06/07): "he estimates he swam about 28 km."
  Source: https://www.thetimes.com/uk/transport/article/adventurer-steps-foot-on-british-soil-38nbhwwk3
  (paywalled; text verified via search extract and the Mezha relay below).
  Relay, Mezha, published 2026-10-12, explicitly citing The Times:
  "Bushby estimated that he covered about 28 km in the water."
  https://mezha.net/eng/news/ccde4014_karl_bushby_reaches_britain/
- **Crossing span: 34 km (21 miles)** — the width crossed, NOT the swum path.
  The Times feature: "20 hours over three days to swim the 21 miles (34km)."
  Full text read at https://thebullpost.press/some-things-are-worth-dying-for-karl-bushbys-incredible-28-year-walk-home-through-deserts-kidnappers-and-polar-bears/
  Wire copies that call it an "approximately 34-kilometre swim" (e.g. AP-derived
  items, and 360.ru, 2026-11-04, "заплыв через Ла-Манш протяженностью 34 километра")
  are using the span figure as the swim distance — weaker usage, do not copy.
- Mode: **swimming** (self-powered, on his back, wetsuit/fins/hand paddles/
  flotation vest; his own word for the technique is "floatimming"). ~20 hours in
  the water over three days, four-hour sections, slept on the support boat,
  restarted from his last position each morning. ExplorersWeb (2026-10-06,
  "Karl Bushby is Back in the UK After Swimming the Channel") confirms the
  schedule and the restart discipline but gives NO distance figure.
- No GPS-track figure exists in any source found. The defensible "actual
  distance swum" is **≈28 km (his estimate)**; 34 km is the documented span.
- Caution: the same Times feature repeats the already-adjudicated-false Peru
  "doctor" story — do not cite it for anything beyond the swim figures.

## (b) Atrato River, Colombia, 2001 — distance NOT FOUND; mode is DRIFTING

No source found states a kilometre figure for the four-day Atrato segment.
Every description — including Bushby's own — calls it floating/drifting,
not swimming:

- Bushby himself, BBC interview relayed by SIA, published 2024-07-16:
  "a few days floating down the Atrato River"; in the same interview he lists
  the Atrato float, the Bering crossing and "navigating the Aldan River in
  Russia" as his prior water experience, adding: "I'm definitely not a
  swimmer, nor do I like swimming."
  https://sia.az/en/news/interesting/1172811.html
- Men's Journal (2013): "floats along the Atrato River for four days",
  "camouflaging himself under a mat of branches" (soda bottles in his vest
  for buoyancy; paramilitary boats passing feet from his head).
  https://www.mensjournal.com/travel/karl-bushby-is-walking-around-the-world-no-really-20130514
- Mirror feature (Gill Swain, 2001, via The Free Library): "For four days he
  floated down the River Atrato disguised as debris."
- The book itself (Giant Steps, ch. 5, quoted in the OnTheGoSolo review,
  2018-08-10): "I drift near the bank, where I am better camouflaged."
  https://onthegosolo.com/giant-steps/
- His official site's Darién chapter: "disguised himself as floating debris,
  drifting downriver among the mangroves." https://karlbushby.world/
- Bushby's own retrospective interview (thelongestwayhome.com) names the
  endpoint emotionally — "The day I reached Riosucio on the Atrato River" —
  still with no distance.
  https://www.thelongestwayhome.com/blog/great-modern-travelers/interview-with-karl-bushby/

Entry point of the float is not pinned in any retrieved source, so no
geographic back-calculation is legitimate. Do NOT put a number on this
segment in the script, and do NOT fold it into a "swam" （游） wording — on
every account, including his own, it is a drift.

## Arithmetic — does "about 300 km" survive?

### (i) Swim-only reading (defensible, sourced)

| Segment | km | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Caspian 2024 | 288 | verified corpus |
| Channel 2026 | ≈28 | Bushby's own estimate, The Times |
| **Total** | **≈316** | |

Variant: Channel counted at the 34 km crossing span → **322 km**. This
matches Backpacker Magazine's independent sum (carousel, 2026-11-04):
"swam roughly 200 miles total" (179 mi Caspian + 21 mi Channel ≈ 322 km).

**Verdict: "约 300 公里是游过来的" SURVIVES the swim-only reading** — on the
two big swims alone, and it is if anything slightly conservative. Script-safe
phrasings: "光里海和英吉利海峡，他就游了 310 多公里" or keep "约 300 公里"
as a round, defensible floor.

### (ii) All-water-movement reading

316 km (above) + Atrato 4-day drift (distance unmeasured in every source) +
Aldan crossing (unmeasured) + unnamed Darién river floats/swims (unmeasured)
+ Bering leads (unmeasured). The true total is strictly greater than 316 km
but **cannot be stated as a number** — three of its components have no
sourced distance anywhere. The claim survives only as a floor
("至少 316 公里，另有三段无测量的水路段"), and the Atrato must never be
counted under the word 游： it was drifting, by his own account.

### Origin note (useful for the episode)

The "300 km" figure circulating in media began as the Caspian PLAN: BBC via
SIA, 2024-07-16 — "planning a 186-mile (300km) swim across the Caspian Sea."
The actual Caspian came in at 288 km; the Channel swim (28 km) then carried
the true swum total back over 300. So the script's number is right for a
different reason than the one usually printed — worth one line on screen.
