# Teardown 007: Karl Bushby on Douyin — Three Treatments of One Walk

Audit date: 2026-11-06. Scope: the three Douyin treatments of
Karl Bushby's completed walk (Punta Arenas, Chile, 1 Nov 1998 to
Hull, England, 2 Nov 2026), checked line by line against the
11-round verified fact base kept in this repo (the Bushby report
under reports/ and the leg-by-leg research notes under
research_notes/). Engagement figures are as observed on
2026-11-06.

## The three works

- A. Xiaoqiao Dashijie, story-serial episode 37 (12:56,
  published 2026-10-10, while Bushby had landed in England but
  had not yet reached home): 84k likes, 2,057 comments, 13k
  saves, 25k shares. Original narration over stock and archive
  visuals; a full biography recap wrapped in a "latest progress"
  news peg.
- B. Empire Museum (17:49, published 2026-11-05, after the
  finish): 328 likes, 73 saves. A re-edit of a foreign TV
  documentary with subtitled interview footage of Bushby
  himself; the title is a single unpunctuated data dump.
- C. Iwanau, Borrow a Life episode 25 (3:23, the series' fixed
  runtime, published 2026-11-04): 4,919 likes, 561 saves,
  612 shares. The second-person life-simulation format applied
  to a living, documented subject; below his own series average
  (a fictional-villainess episode in the same series drew 19k).

## What the data supports

- Distribution follows thesis and narrative, not footage.
  B holds the most primary material (the subject on camera,
  correcting an interviewer on a pack weight) and loses by two
  orders of magnitude: no hook, no argument, no serial peg.
- A wins on structure, not accuracy: a progress-peg cold open,
  the subject's own 1998 plan read out as a contract (8 stages,
  15 km a day, 58,000 km, "at most 12 years"), and every later
  delay measured against that contract. Its single thesis line
  ("the hardest thing to cross is not desert, snowfield or
  strait, but a border") is quoted back verbatim by its top
  comment (4,705 likes) — the thesis landed.
- The fixed 3:23 container caps C on an event topic: the life
  becomes a highlight reel, and the format's machinery (dice,
  chance cards, death and restart) is absent — a real biography
  cannot branch. What remains is the wrapper: second person at
  the title and the closing mirror question, plus the HUD
  corner labels (year, age, place; "tonight you sleep: on the
  ice / in a detention cell / on someone's sofa"; "visa: 40
  days left" as a resource bar).
- All three travel by share, not saves (save-to-like 11-15%):
  this is story distribution, and none of the three converts
  it into a knowledge asset a viewer would keep.

## Fact-check deltas (where the verified base beats all three)

- The rule itself. The subject's own wording bans transport
  that ADVANCES the journey; returns, deportation flights and
  resupply do not breach it. A and B flatten this to "never
  used any transport"; only C keeps the word "advance". All
  three then leave the rule untested — no boundary cases.
- The "Peruvian doctor" is a myth of the secondary literature:
  the memoir records a Trujillo illness hosted by a farm
  manager and his wife, a blood panel (all disease tests
  negative, haemoglobin 11.4), and no doctor anywhere in the
  book. A and C both repeat the doctor; only the primary
  source corrects it.
- China and Bukhara are erased by all three. He entered
  Xinjiang in 2017, was detained, and was escorted out on
  foot after embassy intervention (A claims he "decided not
  to enter China" at all). The 1,000-mile Bukhara-to-Caspian
  walk is missing everywhere: verified dating is departure
  from Bukhara 24 Sep 2023 (his own geotagged post), Caspian
  coast reached in 2024 (the co-walker's own account), swim
  Aug-Sep 2024.
- Russia chronology: the five-year ban was imposed in March
  2013 (for a 2011 border-zone violation), not after Crimea
  as A implies; it was lifted 23 Oct 2014 after the protest
  walk reached the Russian Embassy in Washington on
  10 Sep 2014. The 2006 Bering detention runs 54 days counted
  landing-to-departure (about 31 Mar to 24 May 2006, including
  some 19 days of exit formalities after the 5 May court
  reversal) — no treatment attempts the arithmetic.
- Country counts 25 / 29 / 30 / 31 all circulate (official
  site, BBC-ITV, AP, and a country-by-country sum); A uses 25
  and 29 in different breaths, B takes 30, C takes 29, and
  none flags that the number has no settled source.
- Exclusive to the verified base, used by nobody yet: the
  Yakutsk ferry adjudication (a 2016 tour blog bundles a
  roadside meeting south of Neryungri into its Yakutsk post;
  the ferry-deck photograph shows his trailer was NOT aboard),
  and the correction of the old Wikipedia claim placing him in
  Yakutsk on 15 May 2015 (he was in Berlin; first Lena ferry
  arrival was 24 June 2016).

## The reusable method

1. Plan-as-contract (from A). Read the subject's original
   plan as a numbered contract in the setup; spend the rest
   of the runtime auditing reality against it. The gap
   (12 planned years vs 28) becomes the engine.
2. One thesis line, repeated by structure (from A). "Borders,
   not nature" is never argued twice in words; every episode
   beat is another instance of it.
3. HUD compression (from C). Year-age-place labels plus one
   status line per beat can carry a 28-year life in 3:23
   without narration filler — a container worth borrowing for
   any long-timeline subject.
4. Mirror ending (from C) — with a warning: C now owns the
   homecoming mirror question ("when did you last go home?")
   for this subject on this platform. A second use reads as
   imitation; an own episode must close in a different lane.

## The open lane (candidate for a future topic pool)

Nobody has made the rule itself the topic. The verified base
supports a mechanism episode: open on the apparent breaches
(deportation flight, flights back to resume points, sleeping
on a support boat mid-swim), state the actual test (did the
body get moved forward?), then run the boundary cases —
Panama (released, he travelled back south to re-walk the
escorted segment), Tulcan (bus back to re-walk a missed
stretch), Bogota visa flight, the 2006 deportation, the
Yakutsk ferry question, the swim support boats — and close
on the time ledger the comment sections of all three works
are already asking for: the Bering Strait held him 14 days;
visas held him 11 years. Audience demand is pre-verified:
"does a police car count as a car?" appears under C, and
"was he sent back, so his progress ran backwards?" under A.
End shape to lock at pitch: audience adjudication of the
boundary cases, not the homecoming mirror.
