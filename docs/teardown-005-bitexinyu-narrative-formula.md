# Teardown 005 - Bitexinyu: what travels and how it is told

Part of `docs/teardown-005-creator-bitexinyu.md`. Derived from the 22-video
Bilibili data and the 11 watched videos (`docs/teardown-005-bitexinyu-top-11.md`).
Group averages use a manual grouping; one video can sit in several groups.

## The formula in one line
A viral topic here = a pain point nearly everyone has lived + a
counter-intuitive explanation of it + named, checkable hard-science evidence
+ an emotional reconciliation at the end. Drop any one of the four and
performance falls visibly. Universal experience plus counter-intuition alone
reads as a platitude; the named science (a Nobel, an experiment with a year
and a name, a formula) is what makes viewers feel "this is true, I learned
something" and so save and forward it.

The three strongest lanes, in plain terms:
- Regret and "almost" (Silver Medal Tears 4.76M, the 37% rule): everyone
  has one and most needs consoling.
- Inner friction in relationships (Razor - the unanswered message;
  Aftermath - the cold war; I love you x1000 - routine; wind - being
  different): the strongest motive to forward to a partner or friend.
- News plus a spectacle (the Nobel optogenetics and neutrino videos):
  time-limited traffic, paired with an image like "a blind man sees again" or
  "65 billion particles through you".

### What the hits share
1. One concept per video, and the concept has a name (37% rule, entropy,
   Occam's razor, tit for tat). "Catch" packed in seven concepts and its
   favourite ratio fell to 2.5%.
2. The title carries half the answer: a number, a formula or a paradoxical
   claim (0.8^5 = 33%, the 1000th time, no wind will rise), so viewers click
   to check it.
3. The opening is a lived pain point or a counter-intuitive number (the
   metro missed by 3 seconds, 65 billion neutrinos a second), never a
   definition.
4. The middle always has a real year, person and experiment (1982
   Kahneman, 1902 Hanoi, 1980 Axelrod, 1948 Shannon) to carry credibility.
5. The ending translates the law into one line of self-forgiveness or
   encouragement ("regret has an upper bound, courage does not").
6. News moments must ship within 24 hours: optogenetics went up the day
   after the announcement and has the highest coin ratio on the account
   (2.2%).

### What the misses share
1. Bare film-title headlines (Hooked, Emergence, Learning from Birds,
   Epiphany in the Shower) open no curiosity gap: 846k average, below the
   account mean.
2. Multi-concept compilations or personified explainers (Catch, Brain Arena)
   become lecture lists; like/view drops to 6.5-6.9%.
3. Landings too abstract or too private ("to the you of past, present and
   future"; the principle-method-technique-tool framework): viewers cannot
   retell it in one sentence.
4. Pure economics reasoning with too deep a reversal (the expected-utility
   video, 154k, lowest on the account): more cognitive load than two minutes
   can carry.

Age bias check: later videos have had less time to accumulate. But "Catch"
had 346k after 13 days while Razor had 2.57M after 15, and the age-independent
ratios (like/view, favourite/view) fell in the late period too, so the
decline is not only a timing effect.

## How it is told: the six-step structure
1. **First 5 seconds: a concrete scene or a counter-intuitive number** -
   "second place cannot smile", "he has not replied for three hours", "65
   billion particles through your fingernail". The viewer either sees
   themselves at once or has to know.
2. **Bring in a named piece of science** - an experiment, a person or a
   formula: Kahneman's flight experiment, Axelrod's program tournament,
   0.8^5 = 33%.
3. **Draw the concept** - black ground, minimal motion graphics, one glance
   to understand.
4. **Put it back into life** - apply the scientific result directly to the
   viewer's own pain point. Early or late does not matter; there must be
   one explicit turn.
5. **Reframe the regret or anxiety as normal, even good** - "the optimum
   itself contains regret", "silver-medal tears are the brain drafting the
   next attempt".
6. **End on "to everyone who..."** - a dedication that hands the viewer a
   reason to forward, in effect pre-writing their share caption. The
   alternative ending is a tonight-sized action ("tonight, send the first
   wave"; "today, say one thing they cannot guess").

### Production specification (observed)
- Length: 10 of 11 run 2:39-2:50; neutrinos (3:19) is the one exception.
- Sound: no voice narration in any of them (voice detection about 0 s); the
  captions are the script, over music.
- Caption pace: 36-65 lines per video, a new line every 2.5-4 s (about
  14-24 per minute); Razor slowest, Gaming the score densest.
- Cuts: only 4-10 hard cuts per video; progress comes from animated
  transitions on one dark stage; about 14-24 beats per minute counting
  caption or graphic changes.
- Look: black or deep-blue ground plus one accent colour (gold, red, cyan)
  in code-drawn motion graphics - counters, curves, grids, particles, game
  HUDs, chat bubbles. Direction of the Wind is the only realistic,
  cinematic one.
- Number anchor: each film carries at least one number through the whole
  piece - 0.01 s, 0.2 ms, 0.8^5, 65 billion, the bit readout, 4 lines of
  code, 37%, 0.2 s.

### Title templates
- A: film title plus a paradoxical sentence ("Silver Medal Tears | the worst pain is not failing, it is almost").
- B: film title plus one sentence and a formula ("Razor: every maybe has a price: 0.8^5 = 33%").
- C: an everyday sentence plus a number plus a paradoxical conclusion ("Say I love you for the 1000th time and it carries almost zero information").
- D: "Understand X, and you forgive Y" (entropy).
- News: "This year's Nobel went to [plain metaphor]: what [technical term] actually is".
