# Topic Pool 002: Batch 2, Chosen to Leave the Strip-Mined Set

Written 2026-10-29, after the Batch 1 prior-work check
(`docs/topic-pool-001.md`). Batch 1's lesson: every obvious
"phone principle" topic was attempted by the contest crowd in
the same two weeks. Batch 2 therefore changes territory on
purpose: body, city, optics, transport, and the season. These
topics need a different visual grammar than a circuit board,
which is exactly what the crowd underweights.

Gate key as in pool 001: E everyday, Y "you" question,
J followable journey, S sourceable numbers, F library style fit.
Prior-work status for every entry below: check running on
2026-10-29, verdicts to be appended to this file.

## The six finalists

### 1. The car makes you sick, your phone makes it worse

- Working hook: "You look at your phone in a moving car and feel
  sick in minutes. Your eyes say you are still. Your inner ear
  says you are moving. Your brain believes it has been poisoned."
- Journey: into the inner ear: three fluid loops that report
  motion, contradicted by a still page, resolved by the brain's
  oldest alarm. The driver never gets sick is the closing proof:
  the driver's eyes and ears agree.
- Style: Microscope Story or Data Physicalization (body interior).
- Gate: E yes, Y yes, J yes, S yes, F yes.

### 2. Where the subway wind comes from

- Working hook: "You stand on the platform. The train is not
  there yet, but its wind arrives first. The train has been
  pushing a wall of air down the tunnel for a kilometer."
- Journey: ride the air column ahead of the train through the
  tunnel (the piston effect), watch it spill onto the platform,
  then see why station screen doors changed the wind.
- Style: Satellite Briefing's city grammar, or Engineering
  Briefing Board.
- Gate: E yes (any metro commuter), Y yes, J yes, S yes, F yes.
- Note: the most local topic in either pool; a Shanghai rider
  has felt this thousands of times without ever naming it.

### 3. The mirror swaps left and right, but never up and down

- Working hook: "You raise your right hand. The person in the
  mirror raises their left. Why does the mirror swap left and
  right, but never up and down?"
- Journey: follow one ray and one label into the glass; the
  resolution is that the mirror swaps front and back, and the
  left-right story is one the viewer tells themselves. The
  closing test: text on a T-shirt, readable in the mirror only
  when the shirt faces it.
- Style: Data Storytelling or Whiteboard Explainer.
- Gate: E yes, Y yes, J yes (a paradox, resolved step by step),
  S yes (geometry, not statistics), F yes.
- Note: the most comment-war-friendly topic in the pool;
  everyone believes they already know, and most are wrong.

### 4. The minute after the lights go out

- Working hook: "You turn off the light and for a moment you
  are blind. Thirty minutes later, the same room is visible.
  Your eyes were rebuilding a chemical the whole time."
- Journey: into the retina: the pupil opens in seconds (the
  small fix), then rod cells slowly regenerate rhodopsin (the
  real one). Two clocks, one fast and one slow, explain why
  adaptation has two speeds.
- Style: Microscope Story; the dark palette is this format's
  home ground.
- Gate: E yes, Y yes, J yes, S yes, F yes.

### 5. Trains trust you without a seatbelt. Planes do not.

- Working hook: "You ride a train at 300 km/h with no seatbelt,
  then buckle up in a plane that is barely moving on the
  ground. One of these industries did the math differently."
- Journey: follow the deceleration in both accidents that
  matter: a train's long, survivable braking versus an
  aircraft's sudden turbulence and rejected takeoff; the belt
  solves the plane's problem and creates the train's.
- Style: Engineering Briefing Board or Data Storytelling.
- Gate: E yes, Y yes, J yes, S yes, F yes.
- Note: a contrast topic, the shape the channel's topic rules
  favor; handle respectfully, no crash footage, all schematic.

### 6. Winter is coming, and the doorknob knows

- Working hook: "You touch the doorknob in winter and it bites
  you with thousands of volts. In summer the same charge just
  leaves quietly. The difference is in the air, not in you."
- Journey: electrons collect on the body through the day; dry
  winter air cannot carry them away, humid summer air can; the
  spark is the backlog leaving at once.
- Style: Data Physicalization or Sci-fi Hologram HUD.
- Gate: E yes, Y yes, J yes, S yes, F yes.
- Note: seasonal timing is the asset here: pitched at the end
  of October, this topic's everyday moment is about to happen
  to every viewer daily for three months.

## Reserves, not checked this round

- **Airplane food tastes worse on purpose of physics.** Cabin
  pressure and dry air cut sweet and salt perception by roughly
  a third; umami survives, which is why tomato juice sells in
  the sky.
- **Hot water can freeze faster than cold.** The Mpemba effect:
  real, contested, and only usable with the controversy stated
  on screen; fact card must be unusually careful.
- **A yawn is contagious, a laugh is worse.** Social contagion
  in the brain; psychology slot.
- **Onions make you cry with a chemical factory.** A gas built
  in the cut cells rides to the eyes and turns to acid there.
- **Dreaming of running, and the legs will not move.** Sleep
  paralysis is a feature: the body is switched off on purpose
  during dreams.

## What changed versus Batch 1, and why

- No topic here is a "phone principle". The phone appears only
  as the trigger in finalist 1.
- Four of six topics live in the body or the city, where the
  contest crowd's circuit-board grammar does not transfer; the
  execution bar is higher, which is the moat.
- One topic (6) is timed to the season instead of to a device
  release or a contest theme.

## Prior-work check (Douyin, 2026-10-29)

Method: Douyin web search, video tab; figures are likes shown on
search cards, not views. Verdict scale: (a) a hit owns the topic,
(b) exists but weak or differently angled, (c) basically
untouched. All six finalists scored (b); they differ sharply in
how weak the occupation is.

1. **Motion sickness in a car — (b), window narrowing.** The
   exact spine (phone in car, eyes still versus inner ear moving,
   brain concludes poisoned) already has a small wave of
   vibe-style posts from the last two weeks, all tiny (single
   digits to 141 likes). A broader "principles plus remedies"
   version already reached 24k likes. Viable only fast, or
   re-angled onto the driver-never-sick spine.
2. **Subway piston wind — (b), weakest occupation in the
   batch.** Every existing explainer sits between 17 and 411
   likes; the highest is a four-year-old official ventilation
   video on a broader angle. Two recent AI schematic attempts
   exist (17 and 72 likes), but no video combines this topic,
   the vibe dark-schematic style and the contest tag. The
   vibe-style single-point slot is open.
3. **Mirror left-right — (b), but the resolution itself is
   occupied.** A top science account's talking-head explainer
   stating the full front-back answer holds 397k likes; the
   "aha" is no longer an information gap. Vibe attempts exist
   but are tiny (225 likes at best). Downgraded: remaining
   space is execution or a side angle such as selfie asymmetry.
4. **Dark adaptation — (b).** Two very recent vibe attempts
   (6 and 56 likes, 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-25) did not take
   off; the closest one's summaries never surfaced the
   roughly 30-minute rhodopsin figure. Other results are
   children's animation, medical classroom, exam prep or
   fiction. Room to differentiate with the complete two-clock
   story as an in-retina journey.
5. **High-speed rail versus plane seatbelts — (b); the vibe
   full-comparison niche is near-untouched.** The general
   topic has an 846k-like talking-head hit (2024), but direct
   comparison videos sit at 121 to 1,804 likes, and the only
   two vibe-style attempts on the full comparison are days old
   at 6 and 3 likes. One already uses the 1999 Finland trial
   story, so the facts are known; the execution got no
   traction.
6. **Winter static shocks — (b), precise angle open.** Head
   results run on other angles: fabric and polyester (59k
   likes) and a general how-it-forms plus prevention explainer
   (15k). The tens-of-thousands-of-volts hook appears in one
   small AI series episode (1,498 likes). No video makes "dry
   air cannot carry the charge away, so the voltage keeps
   building" its headline argument, and no vibe-style hit
   exists on the topic. Late-October timing is seasonally
   ideal.
