# Teardown 006: Iwanau (Douyin) — Borrow a Life and the Book of Chance

Audit date: 2026-10-30. Scope: all 45 works on the account
(199k followers, about 3.8 million total likes), top 8 works
frame-checked in depth. Full data table is kept in the working
archive outside this repo.

## Who he is

A vibe-knowledge creator about five weeks into the format at
audit time: 43 videos in 34 days (2026-09-27 to 2026-10-30),
plus 2 image posts from 2023. Three named series carry the
account: a second-person "borrow a life" history series
(16 episodes), a probability and game-theory series
(12 episodes), and a frontier-science series (5 episodes),
plus two small history and psychology series and 8 one-offs.

His production is code-drawn flat vector animation, disclosed
in his own copy as AI-assisted. Audio could not be captured in
this audit, so this teardown makes no claims about his voice
or music.

## What the data supports

- Concentration: the top 3 works hold about 46% of total likes;
  the top 8 are all above 120k likes, rank 9 drops to 81k.
- The chance series is the save and share engine: a probability
  episode saved at a 21% save-to-like ratio; a matching-algorithm
  episode is the account's most shared work (118k shares).
- Timing multiplies: a Nobel-week physics episode published on
  announcement day reached a 72% share-to-like ratio; two
  same-week physics episodes on less familiar topics landed at
  roughly one sixth of its likes.
- Sequels decay: the flagship sci-fi subject at 295k likes, its
  spin-off at 5.2k; the matching hit at 421k, its sequel at 75k.
- Abstract personification is his weakest form: an episode
  personifying an emotion drew 2.5k likes against 716k for a
  concrete object's life story in the same series.
- Off-series one-offs underperform series episodes of similar
  craft (mostly 2k-6k likes).
- Duration is not a lever: hits and flops share 2:10-3:23.

## The reusable method

1. Topic pipelines with gates. A life story must be a concrete
   object with a long lifespan, at least five fate turns, and an
   unresolved end. A chance topic must be a named paradox or
   experiment that can be drawn in frame 1, flips exactly one
   variable, and maps to a daily-life pain in one step. A
   frontier-science topic must carry one body-scale number and,
   ideally, a timing window.
2. Script skeletons. Open on the present, the experiment, or the
   ending — never on a birth or a definition. Show the phenomenon,
   flip the variable, name the concept only after it has been
   seen, then map it to today in one step. Historical anecdotes
   gain a second act by staging the source dispute itself.
3. Endings are chosen from a menu of five — a borrowed historical
   verdict, the decision handed to the viewer, an on-screen
   x-of-3 action checklist, a wish reduction for cosmic topics,
   a classical reframe for technology history — exactly one per
   episode, locked before the middle is written.
4. Persistent information devices. Each episode fixes two or
   three devices for its whole runtime — a year-and-place tag,
   an age counter, an A/B direction pill, an x-of-3 checklist,
   a formula board, a skeuomorphic app screen — in fixed screen
   positions. The diagram carries the argument, scenes carry the
   emotion, and no stretch of pure scene runs longer than about
   15 seconds without an information beat.
5. Packaging. Titles use four formulas (second-person fate
   reversal, concept plus why-question, result-first, body-scale
   question); the publishing description doubles as a compressed
   synopsis of the script.

## What we adopt, and what we do not

Adopted into `docs/baseline-workflow.md` as the Step 0.5 episode
start checklist: the device sheet, the 15-second information-beat
grid, show-then-name order, the locked single ending, staged
disputes, and the synopsis description.

Not adopted: his topics (the pipelines are the asset, not the
subjects), his flat house style (this project keeps its own
styles), his daily posting pace, and any factual looseness —
where his numbers are disputed he sometimes stages the dispute,
but this project's fact card rule is unchanged.
