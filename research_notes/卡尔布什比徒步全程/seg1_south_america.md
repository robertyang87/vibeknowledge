# Segment 1: South America, from Punta Arenas to the Darién Gap and Panama (Nov 1998 – Mar 2001)

Research date: 2026-11-05. These notes add to `early_stages_to_bering.md` and do not repeat it. Where a fact is already there (rules, $800/$500 start money, 45° wind lean, the Seattle Times/AP "four days swimming" line), I only cross-reference it.

## Access notes

- **karlbushby.world (official site):** WebFetch returned HTTP 429 every time. Facts from the site come from search-engine extracts limited to that domain. They are the site's own (self-reported) wording, but I could not read the full page.
  - The `/story` page is the main source. The homepage carries dated journal excerpts.
- **Giant Steps (2006):** The archive.org copy is access-restricted, so I have only its metadata.
  - Chapter list: "Setting out: Punta Arenas and Argentina -- Chile -- Peru -- Ecuador and Southern Columbia -- Darien Gap -- Central America -- ..." ([archive.org metadata](https://archive.org/details/giantstepsameric0000bush)).
  - The book confirms the order Argentina → Chile. It is 324 pages, published by Time Warner Books, London.
  - A second edition exists: "Giant Steps: The Remarkable Story of the Goliath Expedition From Punta Arenas to Russia", Sphere, ISBN 9780751536959 ([AbeBooks](https://www.abebooks.com/9780751536959/Giant-Steps-Remarkable-Story-Goliath-0751536954/plp)).

  - BBC and Guardian coverage from 1999–2001.

  - La Prensa (Panama) and an El Tiempo archive item from 2000–01.

## 2. Route details by country

### Punta Arenas and Argentina (Nov 1998 – 1999)

**Start:**
- He flew out from an RAF base near Oxford and started on 1 Nov 1998 carrying "less than $1,000" ([NY Post via Yahoo, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/articles/daddy-home-man-left-family-191641586.html)).
- The start sum is given variously as $500 ([AP](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/28-years-walking-karl-bushby-050635369.html); [Outdoor Magazyn interview](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-28-year-walk/): "I left with 500 dollars and no support whatsoever"), "a few hundred dollars" (karlbushby.world), and $800 (Backpacker). **Conflict.**

**Wind:**
- "hurricane-force winds on the Patagonian Steppe" ([AP](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/28-years-walking-karl-bushby-050635369.html)).
- The site says gales "ripped him and his hundred-pound gear cart, christened The Beast, clean off the road" ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story)), and describes the wind as tearing him off the road "for days on end".

**Hunger:**
- Men's Journal (2013) reports "near-starvation in Patagonia" and an attempt to poach livestock "to comical effect" ([Men's Journal](https://www.mensjournal.com/travel/karl-bushby-is-walking-around-the-world-no-really-20130514), via search extract; the page returned 403).
- A podcast description mentions surviving on "road scraps and berries" ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/walking-the-world-karl-bushbys-27-year-expedition/id1702002645?i=1000722761150)).

**Argentina:**
- The book's first chapter is "Punta Arenas and Argentina" ([archive.org](https://archive.org/details/giantstepsameric0000bush)). This implies he went NE from Punta Arenas into Argentine Santa Cruz, not up Chile's fjord coast. **Inference.**
- **Flag:** Diario Nuevo Día (Río Gallegos) says he passed through Piedra Buena (Santa Cruz) "en 2011" and has a photo with schoolchildren ([link](https://www.eldiarionuevodia.com.ar/locales/despues-de-28-anos-el-hombre-que-paso-por-santa-cruz-esta-por-llegar-a-casa-una-aventura-de-casi-50-mil-km/)).
  - In 2011 Bushby was in Mexico or Russia, so the year is almost certainly wrong. It is probably late 1998 or 1999.
  - If the place is correct, it puts his route on Ruta 3 along the Atlantic coast (Río Gallegos → Piedra Buena). **Inference.**

### Chile (1999)

- He found that "the high-desert plateau east of the Andes could not sustain the expedition". He crossed the range toward the coast, reached Chile's Pan-American Highway, and walked "north through Santiago, then into the Atacama Desert" ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story), via search extract).
- **Carretera Austral:** No source puts him on it. Given the Argentina-first route, he likely skipped it. **Inference; unconfirmed.**
- From Backpacker (already in the prior notes):
  - An ice-covered tent on a winter night in the Andes.
  - More than 6,000 ft climbed in 3 hours.
  - A view over the Atacama plateau.
  - "Long waterless stretches in Chile's northern deserts".
  - **Inference:** Winter in the Andes on a northbound timeline points to about June–August 1999. The crossing from Argentina to Chile probably happened in the southern winter of 1999.
- He named "Colombia, Chile and the parched Atacama Desert" as his favourite scenery ([NY Post/Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/articles/daddy-home-man-left-family-191641586.html)).
- Pace: the site says he covered more than 2,000 km from Santiago to the Peru border by ~1 Nov 1999.

### Peru (~Nov 1999 – 2000)

- **Arequipa mob:** "attacked by a mob in Arequipa" ([Telegraph via Yahoo, Eric Williams, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/non-ex-paratrooper-march-around-205205826.html)). AP says only "a mob attacked him in Peru" ([AP](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/28-years-walking-karl-bushby-050635369.html)). I found no details of the cause.
- **Wrist:** He stitched a wrist wound himself. This comes from secondary summaries only ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/karl-bushby-completes-world-walk-returns-hull-1829797), via search extract). Not verified in full text.
- **Illness:** "nursed back to health from a bad stomach infection by a Peruvian doctor's family" ([AP](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/28-years-walking-karl-bushby-050635369.html)). IOL calls it his one serious illness, cared for by "a doctor he met in Peru" ([IOL](https://iol.co.za/ios/news/2025-12-07-karl-bushby-the-man-on-a-49-900km-walk-from-south-america-to-england/)).
- **Dust storm:** "a dust storm in Peru" ([Men's Journal 2013](https://www.mensjournal.com/travel/karl-bushby-is-walking-around-the-world-no-really-20130514), via search extract).
- **Donkey:** "In northern Peru he retired The Beast and bought a donkey" ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story)).
  - **Conflict with Backpacker:** Backpacker places the cart in the Andes. This fits, because the cart was used in Chile and southern Peru.
  - **Conflict with the Beast's later history:** The site says that in León, Nicaragua, he welded "The Beast 2", which he still has ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story)).

### Ecuador (2000)

- "The donkey gave way to a horse in Ecuador". Together they climbed "more than nine thousand feet through the Andes of Ecuador and Colombia" ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story)).
- **Robbery:** "robbed in Quito, Ecuador" ([Telegraph/Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/non-ex-paratrooper-march-around-205205826.html)). Another Telegraph piece says only "robbed in Ecuador" ([Telegraph via AOL, Tom Jeffreys, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.aol.com/articles/ex-paratrooper-28-round-world-185837000.html)).
- Photo caption: "Karl Bushby hiking in Ecuador in 2000" ([IOL](https://iol.co.za/ios/news/2025-12-07-karl-bushby-the-man-on-a-49-900km-walk-from-south-america-to-england/)). Backpacker has a caption "The Andes in Ecuador".

### Colombia (2000 – Feb 2001)

- "by the time he reached Colombia's volatile borderlands, he ditched the animals entirely and walked on with a backpack." He crossed the country "sometimes missing active firefights by a single day on either side" ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story)).
- **Dysentery:** He caught amoebic dysentery "in Medellin" ([Telegraph/Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/non-ex-paratrooper-march-around-205205826.html)).
- **Food:** "In South America I ate all sorts of strange things, from guinea pigs to iguana" ([Outdoor Magazyn interview](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-28-year-walk/)).

**Medellín (1 Nov 2000 – ~mid-Jan 2001):**
- The arrival date is "exactly two years to the day since his first step" (karlbushby.world). His journal calls Medellín "the last [city] I shall pass through in South America".
- **Planning:**
  - About two months, planned "like a military evasion".
  - He interviewed soldiers, police, investigative journalists and survivors.
  - He lived "disguised as a vagrant" because of the kidnap risk.
  - He planned with his father, using contingency routes coded red, blue and black.
  - He signed "a professionally drafted will" at his father's urging.
  - All from [karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story).
- The Outdoor Magazyn feature says "several months" of planning in Medellín, with his father also involved ([link](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/)).

**Catalina Estrada:**
- Wikipedia says they met in Medellín in 2000 (search extract of [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)).
- The site says "for two months, Karl and Catalina worked the problem together" ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story)).
- **Conflict:** Men's Journal places the meeting under a 1999 heading. That contradicts the Nov 2000 Medellín arrival.
- Book reviewers refer to "Catty the girl he falls for in Colombia" ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/976427.Giant_Steps)). One reviewer says "what should have been a fleeting romance turns into a bit of an obsession".
- La Razón (Mexico) says they officially separated in 2011, about 11 years together ([link](https://www.razon.com.mx/mundo/2026/11/02/quien-es-karl-bushby-el-britanico-cruzo-el-mundo-caminando-en-28-anos-para-volver-a-casa/)). This conflicts with Backpacker's 2008 breakup letter (see the prior notes).
- Bushby himself: "it may have been Medellín... because I had a girlfriend there" ([Outdoor Magazyn](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-28-year-walk/)).

## 3. Darién Gap crossing in detail (Jan – Feb 2001)

### Disguise and approach by road

- His first plan was to pass as homeless: dyed hair, darkened skin, rags, and gear hidden in sacks. It "worked only partly". Paramilitary groups stopped him without serious trouble ([Outdoor Magazyn feature](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/)).
- He sewed maps, compasses and medicine into hidden pockets ([Colombia One, 4 Nov 2026](https://colombiaone.com/2026/11/04/karl-bushby-28-year-walk-around-world/)).
- His aim was to avoid being taken for a spy, a mercenary or a FARC member ([La Cuarta](https://www.lacuarta.com/chile/noticia/el-ingles-que-demoro-28-anos-en-dar-la-vuelta-al-mundo-sin-usar-transporte-partio-en-punta-arenas/)). Q'hubo Medellín also mentions the homeless disguise ([link](https://qhubomedellin.com/actualidad/internacional/karl-bushby-camino-28-anos-desde-chile-hasta-inglaterra-HP43084289)).
- About 300 km of road was planned. He waited for the dry season. In 2000–01 the road was effectively a front line, with villages changing hands ([Outdoor Magazyn feature](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/)).
- **Road route:** Medellín → Cañasgordas (19 Jan) → Dabeiba (21 Jan) → Bajirá (28 Jan) ([karlbushby.world](https://karlbushby.world/)).
  - **Inference:** This is the Medellín–Turbo road (the Carretera al Mar) as far as Dabeiba/Mutatá. He then turned west to Belén de Bajirá on the Atrato basin side, not to Turbo.
- **Bajirá, 28 Jan:** An army officer and sergeant made "a final attempt to dissuade" him and wrote an official memo recording the time, date, place and people, which they signed. Bushby says he was "almost physically sick with fear" ([karlbushby.world](https://karlbushby.world/), via search extract).
  - Backpacker says he went "against the advice of the Colombian military".
- Locals expected FARC to kill or kidnap him as a suspected agent of Plan Colombia ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)).
- He was helped by armed groups and the army on the northern roads ([karlbushby.world](https://karlbushby.world/), via search extract).
- He switched to "jungle mode": green uniform, tactical vest and backpack ([Outdoor Magazyn feature](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/)).

### Atrato River

- He floated downstream "for four days... pretending to be a pile of rubbish drifting on the water" ([Outdoor Magazyn interview](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-28-year-walk/)). The prior notes have the AP 2006 "four days swimming down a river in camouflage". ExplorersWeb calls the Atrato "crocodile-laden" ([ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-is-home-after-28-years-on-the-road/)).
- Giant Steps, ch. 5, quoted by a reviewer:
  - "I am constantly on the lookout for crocodiles. Normally it's just a large iguana."
  - "I drift near the bank, where I am better camouflaged."

  ([Onthegosolo review](https://onthegosolo.com/giant-steps/))
- The reviewer says he had to float or swim every river because there are no bridges and he refused transport. They call the Darién the book's best section.
- **3 Feb 2001, La Honda:** A journal entry places him drifting on the Atrato.
  - A search extract also attributes to this entry an encounter with a man in dark-green uniform with an AK-47, a search, and a night in a cell "having no visa".
  - **Flag:** The extract may have merged this with the later Panama arrest. It is unverified.

### Jungle stage

- With a compass he went through roadless jungle toward the mountains. One day's road-equivalent took ten days ([Outdoor Magazyn feature](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/)).
- "Ten days through the trackless jungle brought him to the Panamanian village of Paya: a hot meal and the first real rest in weeks" ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story)).
- FARC had displaced the indigenous people, so there were no trails. In Panama the small Kuna villages were calmer, and there were rivers he could float again ([Outdoor Magazyn feature](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/)).
- **Guides:** No source names a guide. Every account describes a solo, self-navigated crossing.
  - **Inference:** Paya, a Kuna village, is the usual first settlement on the classic Cristales/Palo de las Letras route. He may have had local help there, but this is unconfirmed.
- **Condition:** "I was terribly ill there. I ran out of food and had to survive in that jungle." At the border post "I just cried like a baby" ([Outdoor Magazyn interview](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-28-year-walk/)). The feature says he cried for about 15 minutes near the border.
- He was "at gunpoint a few times" (Guardian quote, relayed by [Colombia One](https://colombiaone.com/2026/11/04/karl-bushby-28-year-walk-around-world/)). He twice nearly avoided capture by FARC (Outdoor Magazyn feature).

### Arrest and jail in Panama

- **Arrest:** On Friday 16 Feb 2001 he walked into Boca de Cupe "in a green uniform and combat vest" and was met at gunpoint by Panamanian police. The confrontation was tense because the police were also fighting guerrillas ([karlbushby.world](https://karlbushby.world/); [Outdoor Magazyn](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/)).
- **Reason:** Various sources give:
  - A restricted border zone during the conflict, with no visa ([karlbushby.world](https://karlbushby.world/); AP).
  - Entry without a visa (Wikipedia).
  - "illegal alien" (Telegraph).
  - The border was "closed due to the war with Colombian guerrillas FARC" ([Dave's Travel Corner interview](https://www.davestravelcorner.com/interviews/karl-bushby-adventurer-and-author/)).
- **Length:** 18 days, in every source.
- **Location (conflicting):**
  - Metetí (Wikipedia, citing Giant Steps).
  - La Palma, with a journal entry dated 23 Feb 2001 and "held with other jailed Latinos" (karlbushby.world, via search extract).
  - "They moved me from one prison to another" (Outdoor Magazyn).
  - "held in jails" (plural) (Dave's Travel Corner).
  - **Inference:** He was moved Boca de Cupe → La Palma (the provincial capital) → possibly Metetí. All may be right.
- **Transfer by helicopter:** His father Keith said: "Cuando lo arrestaron en Panamá, lo llevaron en un helicóptero y lo metieron en la cárcel" ("When they arrested him in Panama, they took him in a helicopter and put him in jail") ([El Tiempo, 8 Aug 2025](https://www.eltiempo.com/cultura/gente/el-hombre-que-quiere-caminar-el-planeta-entero-y-viene-recorriendolo-desde-3479564)).
  - **Flag:** A helicopter transfer raises an "unbroken footsteps" question. He would have had to resume from the arrest point, as his rules require. I found no source on how he resumed after release.
- **Release:**
  - Intelligence officers allowed no contact until a senior regional officer came. Bushby drew what he knew of Colombian positions on maps, using his army intelligence training, and they "were pleased" ([Outdoor Magazyn](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/); [Colombia One](https://colombiaone.com/2026/11/04/karl-bushby-28-year-walk-around-world/)).
  - Released after 18 days, he reached Panama City "a few weeks later". The ordeal "ended only when he walked into the British Embassy in Panama City". One site version says he continued "under a strict escort" (karlbushby.world, via search extract).
  - **Unverified:** A single-source claim that John Prescott or Roman Abramovich helped is probably confused with the 2006 Russia case. Discount it.
- **Total jail time:** Men's Journal 2013 counts "71 days in jail in two different countries", i.e. Panama 18 + Russia ~53. **Inference.**

## 4. The cart, funding, food and sleeping

**The Beast:**
- A hundred-pound gear cart ([karlbushby.world/story](https://karlbushby.world/story)).
- "a modified golf cart" (Goodreads reviewer Janet, [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/976427.Giant_Steps)).
- A "two-wheeled cart" ([ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-is-home-after-28-years-on-the-road/)).
- Retired in northern Peru; a donkey, then a horse, then a backpack from the Colombian border; "The Beast 2" built in León, Nicaragua.

**Funding:**
- No backing before the US border, only "some handouts" ([Dave's Travel Corner](https://www.davestravelcorner.com/interviews/karl-bushby-adventurer-and-author/)).
- Family fundraising later ([IOL](https://iol.co.za/ios/news/2025-12-07-karl-bushby-the-man-on-a-49-900km-walk-from-south-america-to-england/)).
- "The Americas very quickly became survival mode" (IOL).

**Food:**
- "You can survive in Latin America with a pocket full of rice" (Dave's Travel Corner; also Outdoor Magazyn).
- "Gas station food or what locals gave him" (ExplorersWeb).
- "countless meals of just rice and water by people seeking nothing in return" (AP).
- Guinea pig and iguana (Outdoor Magazyn).

**Locals and shelter:**
- "I could not have done South America without the help of locals... fed me, watered me, and even nursed me back to health" (Dave's Travel Corner).
- AP mentions shacks built from car parts.

**Sleeping:** "I slept everywhere humanly imaginable" (Dave's Travel Corner). Tent in the Andes (Backpacker).

**Pace:**
- Early days were about 19 miles/day (IOL). The plan was 20 miles/day and 3,000 miles/year (Goodreads description of the second edition).
- No daily log from the South America leg was found.
- **Inference:** Santiago → Peru border is more than 2,000 km in about 6–8 months.

## 5. Gaps and caveats

- I did not find exact border dates for Argentina↔Chile, Chile→Peru (only "first anniversary"), Peru→Ecuador, or Ecuador→Colombia.
- I found no per-day distances or towns along the Peruvian and Ecuadorian Pan-American Highway, apart from Arequipa and Quito.
- I found no contemporary 1999–2001 press. BBC, Guardian and the Hull Daily Mail archives were unreachable. The Wikipedia citation of the Hull Daily Mail, 16 Sep 1998, p. 3 (pre-departure) is in the prior notes.
- The official site and the book are the primary sources, but I saw only search extracts of the site (HTTP 429). Fetch https://karlbushby.world/story directly later to verify the quotes and to get the full "Into the Wild" year-by-country section.
- The karlbushby.world sponsor page includes an "English Channel swim 5 Oct 2026" claim. It is out of scope here, so check it elsewhere.
