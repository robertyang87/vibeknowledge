**Notes on these anchors:**
- **"Atacama Peru" caption:** A reader comment on the page points out that the Atacama is in Chile. The caption is either mislabelled or refers to the Atacama's northern fringe around Tacna. **[CONFLICT, minor]**
- **"Top of the world":** The Pan-American (E35) in Ecuador peaks at roughly 3,500–4,000 m in the Chimborazo/Riobamba–Ambato corridor, and around Tixán/Alausí and El Tambo/Cañar. "Top of the world" probably marks one of these high passes, or a Chimborazo viewpoint. **[INF]**
- **Extra.ec's "around July":** This is probably derived from the 15 July photo. **[INF]**

### Derived timeline [INF]

**Peru:**
- He entered Peru about 1 Nov 1999 at Tacna.
- **Arequipa** (about 370 km by road, plus about 40 km off the coastal Panamericana Sur, at about 2,300 m) would fall around **mid-to-late Nov 1999**.
- He had to come back down to the coast via the Camaná–Ocoña–Chala stretch of the Panamericana Sur to reach **Nazca** (about 570 km from Arequipa). That is consistent with the caption "2000", i.e. Jan–Feb 2000.
- **Lima** (about 450 km past Nazca) would come around Feb–Mar 2000.
- **Trujillo, Chiclayo and Piura** (Sechura Desert) would follow around Mar–May 2000. **Tumbes / Aguas Verdes–Huaquillas** would come around May–June 2000.
- The site's "in northern Peru he retired The Beast and bought a donkey" puts the cart-to-donkey switch somewhere around Trujillo–Piura, probably spring 2000. **[INF]**

**Ecuador:**
- He would have entered at Huaquillas about June 2000, been at altitude on 15 July 2000, and reached Quito around late July or August 2000.
- He would have reached the border at Tulcán–Rumichaca around August–September 2000.
- That leaves about 6–8 weeks to cover the roughly 800 km from Ipiales to Medellín (Pasto–Popayán–Cali–Medellín) by 1 Nov 2000.
- All of these dates are inferred from road distances and the anchor dates above. None is attested.

**Distance and pace check [INF]:**
- Tacna→Tumbes on the Panamericana is about 2,600–2,700 km, including the Arequipa detour.
- Huaquillas→Rumichaca via Cuenca–Riobamba–Quito is about 800–850 km.
- So Leg B is roughly 3,400–3,600 km in about 10 months. That averages about 11–12 km per calendar day, consistent with long stops for illness and convalescence and for buying animals.
- The official homepage, via a search extract, describes the early days as "thirty kilometres a day" while living on discarded food and roadside forage; this is not specific to Peru. The publisher's plan figure was 20 miles a day ([Little, Brown](https://www.littlebrown.co.uk/titles/karl-bushby/giant-steps/9780751536959/)).
- **No real daily-distance data was found for this leg.**

## 2. Route specifics (Pan-American) [INF unless cited]

**Peru, Panamericana Sur:**
- Tacna → Moquegua → (detour inland) **Arequipa** (attested only as the mob-attack site) → back to the coast at Camaná → Ocoña → Chala → **Nazca** (attested, 2000) → Ica → Pisco → Cañete → **Lima**.
- **Arequipa detour:** The Panamericana Sur bypasses Arequipa via Repartición / La Joya. Being attacked "in Arequipa" therefore means he made a deliberate detour, or the place name is loose. **[INF; CONFLICT possible]** No source explains why he went there; possible reasons are resupply, a consulate, or mail.

**Peru, Panamericana Norte:**
- Lima → Huacho → Chimbote → **Trujillo** → Chiclayo → **Piura** (Sechura Desert) → Sullana → **Tumbes** → Aguas Verdes.
- Not one of these towns is named in any source found. Only the region, "northern Peru", is attested (karlbushby.world).

**Ecuador, E35 "Troncal de la Sierra":**
- Huaquillas → (Machala → Cuenca, or via Loja) → Alausí → Riobamba → Ambato → Latacunga → **Quito** (attested only as the robbery site) → Otavalo → Ibarra → Tulcán → Rumichaca → Ipiales (Colombia).
- The site's "climbed more than nine thousand feet through the Andes of Ecuador and Colombia" fits the Sierra route rather than the coastal Guayaquil route.
- Extra.ec explains the horse switch as needed to cope with altitude above 9,000 ft ("altitud superior a los 9.000 pies"). Its "9,000 ft" is probably a misreading of the site's elevation-gain phrase. **[CONFLICT/paraphrase]**

**Coastal desert:**
- In Peru: "spanned the deserts of Peru" ([Durango Herald, 14 Dec 2013](https://www.durangoherald.com/articles/long-walk-home/)).
- An iconic photo shows Bushby "hands to his chest, face wrapped against the sand, standing in the Peruvian desert" (described by walker Tom Turcich, [AFAR Travel Tales podcast s5 e12](https://afar.com/podcasts/travel-tales/s5-e12-benefits-of-walking)).
- This fits the "dust storm in Peru" item already in seg1 (Men's Journal).

## 3. The switch from cart to donkey to horse to backpack (new angles)

- **Magis (ITESO, Mexico), interview Jan/May 2020:**
  - "Karl se hizo de un burro en Perú; después lo cambió por un caballo en Ecuador, y más tarde se dio cuenta de que prefería viajar solo." ("Karl got himself a donkey in Peru; later he swapped it for a horse in Ecuador, and later still he realised he preferred to travel alone.")
  - The first cart "no llegó muy lejos" (didn't get very far). Instead of building another, he turned to the donkey and then the horse ([Magis, Alejandro Zamora](https://magis.iteso.mx/nota/karl-bushby-hacia-el-horizonte/)).
  - The site gives a different reason for dropping the animals: Colombia's "volatile borderlands". **[CONFLICT, mild]** Magis says personal preference for travelling alone; both may be true.
  - Magis also gives a different origin for Beast 2: it says students in Nicaragua built "The Beast" (the improved version) **for** him. Seg1 has the site's version, that he welded it himself in León. **[CONFLICT, minor]**
- **Extra.ec** says the donkey was "de carga" (a pack donkey), the horse replaced it in the "geografía andina de Ecuador y Colombia", and he went on with only a backpack "hasta la frontera colombiana" (to the Colombian border) ([link](https://www.extra.ec/noticia/mundo/camino-28-anos-chile-reino-unido-2-noviembre-termina-viaje-karl-bushby-166658.html)).
  - **[CONFLICT]** The site says he took the horse *into* the Andes of Colombia and dropped it in the "borderlands". Extra.ec implies he dropped it by the Colombian border.
  - "Borderlands" could mean the Ecuador–Colombia frontier (Nariño/Putumayo) or simply conflict zones further north.
- **Not found:** the donkey's or horse's names, prices, where they were bought or sold, or what happened to The Beast (abandoned, sold or given away).

## 4. Illness, the doctor's family, and the wrist

- **AP (Brian Melley, 31 Oct 2026):** The sentence groups the doctor's family with strangers who helped him. He was "nursed back to health from a bad stomach infection by a Peruvian doctor's family", in the same sentence as shacks "built from car parts" and meals of "rice and water" ([Yahoo CA](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/28-years-walking-karl-bushby-050635369.html)).
- **Plural or singular?** ExpressNews UK (Spanish) has "familias peruanas humilladas [sic, probably *humildes*] que lo cuidaron de infecciones estomacales" ("humble Peruvian families who cared for him through stomach infections", plural) ([link](https://expressnews.uk.com/karl-bushby-regresa-casa-28-anos-caminata-vuelta-al-mundo/)). **[UNVERIFIED; likely a garbled AP rewrite]** It makes both the families and the infections plural.
- **Mezha:** "the family of a Peruvian doctor helping him recover from a severe intestinal infection" ([mezha.net](https://mezha.net/eng/news/65dadd98_karl_bushby_completes_his)).
- **IBTimes UK:** "He stitched a gashed wrist himself and was treated for a stomach infection in Peru" ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/karl-bushby-completes-world-walk-returns-hull-1829797)).
  - This is now verified in full text (seg1 had it from a search snippet only).
  - **Note:** grammatically, "in Peru" may govern only the infection. The wrist injury is **not** firmly placed in Peru, and it is not tied to the Arequipa mob. Linking the two is tempting but **[INF, unsupported]**.
- **Car-door shacks, Peru:** "I've come across people living on the side of the road in Peru, and they've got a shack made out of car doors strung together... They'd just be overwhelmed to feed me a bowl of rice" ([Durango Herald, 14 Dec 2013](https://www.durangoherald.com/articles/long-walk-home/)). This is the Peru-specific origin of AP's "shacks built from car parts".
- **Not found:** the doctor's name, the town, or the length of the convalescence.
  - **[INF]** A long stop of this kind could account for part of the slow average pace in Peru.

## 9. Conflicts summary

1. **Why the animals were dropped:** Colombia's danger (site) vs. preferring to travel alone (Magis 2020).
2. **Where the horse was dropped:** in the Colombian Andes or borderlands (site) vs. before or at the Colombian border (Extra.ec).
3. **"Over 9,000 feet":** cumulative climbing (site) vs. absolute altitude (Extra.ec).
4. **Beast 2:** self-welded in León (site, in seg1) vs. built by Nicaraguan students (Magis).
5. **Illness:** one infection, one doctor's family (AP, Mezha) vs. plural infections and families (ExpressNews, low quality).
6. **Photo caption "Atacama Peru":** geographically wrong, or refers to the Tacna fringe.
7. **Arequipa:** a city off the Panamericana Sur trunk. This implies a detour or a loose place name.
