## 1. Panama (Feb 2001)

**Takeaway:** He came out of the Darién in February 2001 and was jailed for 18 days for having no visa or entry stamp. Sources disagree about the town.

- **Arrest and detention:** Wikipedia: he entered Panama in Feb 2001 and spent "18 days in a jail in Metetí for entering the country without a visa" (citing *Giant Steps*). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby
- **Place of arrest:** [site-snippet] He was "arrested at Boca de Cupe in February 2001 for entering without a visa", spent 18 days in a Panamanian jail, and was released once the authorities understood what he was doing. — https://karlbushby.world/story
  - **Conflict or complement:** Boca de Cupe is a Darién river village. Metetí sits on the Pan-American Highway. INFERENCE: he was arrested at Boca de Cupe and held at Metetí. That would make the two accounts compatible, but no source states it.
- **Reason for the jailing:** The AP 2026 wording is "after crossing a restricted border zone in Panama, he was jailed for 18 days". — https://krmg.com/2026/10/31/in-28-years-of-walking-karl-bushby-has-overcome-jungle-desert-and-red-tape-now-hes-nearly-home/
  - **Conflict:** AP's "restricted zone" versus Wikipedia's "no visa". Both may be true.
- **Darién anecdote:** "Four days swimming down a river in camouflage" while evading FARC (Keith Bushby to AP). — https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump
- **Memoir quote:** "I drift near the bank, where I am better camouflaged. Boats pass all the time." — *Giant Steps*, via https://onthegosolo.com/giant-steps/
- **Diary:** [site-snippet] A 3 Feb 2001 entry is from the Atrato River, Colombia, just before the Panama exit.
- **Photo:** Backpacker has a photo captioned "Paya, Panama". Paya is a Darién village on the old route. — https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon

## 2. Central America (2001): Costa Rica, Nicaragua, Honduras, El Salvador, Guatemala

**Takeaway:** He followed the Pan-American Highway north through all five countries in 2001. **El Salvador is confirmed** by the site's country list and by a 2001 photo caption. The only well-attested event is the welding of "The Beast 2" in León, Nicaragua. Dated entries for Costa Rica, Honduras and Guatemala are a GAP.

- **Route:** [site-snippet] "2001–2002 Panama to Mexico ... pushed north through Costa Rica, Nicaragua, Honduras, El Salvador, Guatemala, and into Mexico." — https://karlbushby.world/story
- **"The Beast 2":** [site-snippet] In León, Nicaragua, "he used a local technical school's workshop to weld" a successor cart, "The Beast 2". It is "so resilient it remains with him today". — https://karlbushby.world/story
  - So the original "Beast" (from Punta Arenas) was replaced here. The cart stolen in Montana in 2003 was therefore Beast 2.
- **El Salvador:** A search-indexed photo caption reads "Karl Bushby in El Salvador in 2001". I saw it only in search results, around a BBC-mirror explainer: https://bbcinsight.pages.dev/news/articles/cp8rd3n80j4o (unverified mirror).
- **Costa Rica:** Backpacker has a photo captioned "Costa Rica", with no text. — https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon
- **Memoir:** Chapter 6 of *Giant Steps* is "Central America". I could not reach the text (archive.org is access-restricted). — https://archive.org/details/giantstepsameric0000bush
- **Reader impression:** A Goodreads reviewer writes that the author's strain starts to show in "Central and North America". — https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1106260

## 3. Mexico (c. Sep 2001 to Apr 2002)

**Takeaway:** He entered from Guatemala, probably via Chiapas on the Pacific side, and walked north-west for about seven months to Nogales, Sonora.

- **Tonalá diary:** [site-snippet] An entry dated 11 Sep 2001 at Tonalá, Mexico, records his reaction to the 9/11 attacks ("feels close to tears"). Tonalá, Chiapas, is on Hwy 200 along the Pacific coast. That Chiapas is meant is my INFERENCE.
- **Exit from Mexico:** Wikipedia gives "On 29 April 2002, Bushby left Mexico and entered the United States at Nogales, Arizona". It cites Mitch Tobin, "Brit walking the world", Arizona Daily Star, 30 Apr 2002, pp. B1, B6.
  - **Conflict:** The site says "In May 2002, Karl reached the United States border at Nogales". Prefer 29 Apr 2002, which the contemporary newspaper supports.
- **Exact route through Mexico:** Unknown (GAP). INFERENCE: a Pacific-side line (Chiapas, Oaxaca, Guerrero/Michoacán, Jalisco, Sinaloa, Sonora, then Nogales) fits the Tonalá and Nogales endpoints. No source confirms the intermediate cities.
- **Photo:** Backpacker has a photo captioned "The Mexico-U.S. Border".
- **Memoir:** Chapter 7 is "Mexico".

## 4. USA (29 Apr 2002 to about Feb 2003)

**Takeaway:** His route ran Nogales, Tucson, Parker (AZ), Las Vegas, then north to Montana. He had a visa crisis in Las Vegas and flew to Bogotá and back. In Shelby, Montana, at New Year 2003, his cart was stolen; he rebuilt in Helena, and the original turned up in a quarry.

- **Tucson and sponsors:**
  - [site-snippet] "Shortly after reaching Tucson, Arizona, gear manufacturer Superfeet joined as a formal sponsor."
  - [site-snippet] In 2002 "a Canadian web hosting company stepped in to manage the expedition website and provided baseline monthly funding". The company is not named in the snippets (GAP).
- **Parker, AZ:** [site-snippet] A diary entry on Thursday 13 Jun 2002 at Parker, Arizona, says he is physically and emotionally drained. He was walking through the Sonoran/Mojave summer heat.
  - Backpacker: "sizzling heat in the Southwestern U.S."
  - The memoir blurb: "the heat of the Nevada desert". — https://books.google.com/books/about/Giant_Steps.html?id=KL3dzQEACAAJ
- **Las Vegas visa expiry (Jul 2002):**
  - Wikipedia: in Las Vegas in July he was told his 90-day visa had expired and could not be renewed while he was in the US. He flew to Bogotá to apply for a six-month visa and resumed at Las Vegas in August.
  - Citations: Mitch Tobin, "Take a hike, U.S. tells global walker", Arizona Daily Star, 26 Jul 2002, pp. A1, A11; Tobin, "Global walker resumes trek's U.S. leg", Arizona Daily Star, 20 Aug 2002, p. A4.
  - Why Bogotá: INFERENCE. Colombia was where Catalina Estrada lived (Medellín); see the earlier notes.
- **Las Vegas to Montana:** Unknown (GAP). INFERENCE: the I-15 corridor through Utah, Idaho and Montana (Helena, Great Falls, Shelby) to the Sweetgrass/Coutts border. This fits Shelby on I-15 and the Salt Lake Tribune coverage, but no source gives the route.
- **Cart theft (New Year 2003):**
  - UPI, 3 Jan 2003: Bushby, 33, "about halfway" through his trek, reached Shelby, Montana. Someone took his 120-pound rolling pack/cart, holding all his gear, from outside the **Four Corners Bar** while he had a drink with locals on Tuesday night (31 Dec 2002). He suspects it was tossed into a pickup.
  - His quote to the Great Falls Tribune: "Shelby has a new community member, because I'll be around until I find this."
  - **Error in UPI:** it says he had walked through "Africa". — https://www.upi.com/Odd_News/2003/01/03/Jockstrip-The-world-as-we-know-it/48711041584400/
  - Wikipedia puts it on New Year's Day 2003, at a bar in Toole County, a day before he was due to enter Canada. He spent about a month restocking, and the cart was found in a quarry just before he left the US. This cites the Salt Lake Tribune via newspapers.com.
  - [site-snippet] The site says "The Beast 2" was stolen from outside a café in Montana near the Canadian border. He "spent a month in Helena building a replacement", and the original was later "found abandoned in a local quarry".
  - **Conflicts:**
    - bar (UPI) vs café (site)
    - night of 31 Dec (UPI "Tuesday night") vs 1 Jan (Wikipedia)
    - Shelby (UPI) vs Toole County (Wikipedia). These agree: Shelby is the Toole County seat.
- **Memoir:** Chapter 8 is "United States".

## 5. Canada (2003 to spring 2004): Alberta, (BC), Yukon

**Takeaway:** Canada is the worst-documented country in this segment.
- **Supported by sources:** winter walking on the Trans-Canada Highway toward Calgary, a resupply at BATUS Suffield, the Calgary Herald story about his partner being refused entry (Apr 2003), and the Alaska Highway at −40.
- **INFERENCE only:** a BC/Yukon transit along the Alaska Highway, via Dawson Creek, Fort Nelson, Watson Lake, Whitehorse and Beaver Creek. That is the only road route to Delta Junction. **No source places him in Whitehorse or Dawson City.**
- **Dawson City:** Probably NOT on his route. Dawson City is off the Alaska Highway, on the Klondike Highway and Top of the World road. The task's mention of "Dawson" may mean Dawson Creek (Mile 0). Flag this.

Findings:
- **Calgary and Suffield:** [site-snippet] He "pushed through a brutal winter on the Trans-Canada Highway toward Calgary", detoured to "a British Army training unit at Suffield for supplies", then "took on the Alaska Highway at around minus forty". — https://karlbushby.world/story
  - INFERENCE: from Coutts the logical line is Medicine Hat, then Suffield (on the Trans-Canada), then west to Calgary, then north via Edmonton and Grande Prairie to Dawson Creek.
  - BATUS is a real unit at CFB Suffield. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_Training_Unit_Suffield
  - No independent source on the BATUS visit was found.
- **Partner refused entry:**
  - Wikipedia notes difficulties bringing Catalina Estrada "particularly while travelling through Canada in 2003".
  - Citation: Colette Derworiz, "Colombian denied entry", Calgary Herald, 11 Apr 2003, p. B8. I have the citation only. It places him in the Calgary area around Mar–Apr 2003 (INFERENCE).
- **Date conflict:** The 2025 Banff-fundraiser article (Rocky Mountain Outlook / Great West syndication) reportedly says "by 2004, he crossed into Canada ... up through Alberta via the Trans-Canada Highway" (search snippet only; page 403).
  - This conflicts with the 2003 theft "a day before crossing into Canada" and the Calgary Herald date of Apr 2003. Prefer early 2003 for the entry into Canada.
  - URLs: https://www.rmoutlook.com/mountain-guide/he-refused-to-quit-fundraiser-for-man-on-27-year-walk-around-the-world-comes-to-banff-11944307 ; https://www.westernwheel.ca/beyond-local/he-refused-to-quit-fundraiser-for-man-on-27-year-walk-around-the-world-comes-to-banff-11969364
  - The 7 Mar 2026 Banff Centre fundraiser "Karl Bushby: Get Him Home!" was hosted by Mark Scholz with guest Sam Roberts. — https://www.banffcentre.ca/karl-bushby-get-him-home
  - This suggests an Alberta support network. INFERENCE: it may date back to his 2003 passage.
- **Alaska Highway, winter 2003–04:**
  - [site-snippet] He walked it "at forty below".
  - Timing INFERENCE: he reached Delta Junction (the highway's end) by 16 May 2004, so he covered the roughly 2,200 km from Dawson Creek in late winter to spring 2004.
- **Baidu error:** Baidu Baike says he met Kieffer "in Canada". This is contradicted by Nexus and the Seattle Times (Alaska, 2005). — https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Karl%20Bushby/3397842
- **Memoir:** Chapter 9 is "Canada and Alaska".

## 6. Alaska, part 1: Delta Junction and Fairbanks to Unalakleet (May 2004 to spring 2005)

**Takeaway:**
- He reached Fairbanks in May 2004 and waited out the summer for the rivers to freeze.
- In Dec 2004 he went west into the Interior on the winter trail.
- He covered about a mile in three days near Dunbar, at a −40 wind chill. He survived still-air temperatures down to −51°C and lost 40 lb.
- He reached Unalakleet before spring 2005 and his visa forced a stop.

Findings:
- **Delta Junction and Fairbanks:**
  - [site-snippet] 16 May 2004, Delta Junction: he arrives exhausted, shuts himself in a motel room and refuses to get back on the road.
  - [site-snippet] "In May 2004, Karl walked into Fairbanks, Alaska — the absolute end of the paved road", where "he had to stop and wait for the rivers to freeze into a road north" (i.e. west). Crossing the marshy tundra in summer was impossible. — https://karlbushby.world/
- **Year conflict:** ExplorersWeb, Alaska Story and Baidu say he "reached Alaska in 2005". The site's dated diary (May 2004) is more specific. Prefer 2004.
  - https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/
  - https://thealaskastory.com/world-walker-the-explorer-who-walked-across-alaska-and-the-globe-for-nearly-three-decades-nears-end-of-journey/
- **Funding during the Fairbanks pause:** [site-snippet] His father Keith secured a book agent. "A publisher's advance [Time Warner] allowed Karl to re-equip and train for an entirely new environment."
- **Equipment change:** He went from wheeled cart to pulled sled (pulk) for winter. Sources mention the sled but not its make.
  - Pre-strait prep, per Baidu (low reliability): a specially made suit, practice entering the water, 182 kg of dehydrated food, two lightweight sleds.
  - The 2006 crossing used dry/immersion suits, about 200-lb sleds (Backpacker), and night-vision scopes and a parafoil kite camera (PlanetFear via climbing.de). — https://www.climbing.de/news/bering-straits-crossed-on-foot-38956.html
- **Winter 2004–05 departure and Dunbar:**
  - [site-snippet] "In December 2004 he set out from Fairbanks into the Alaskan interior. He returned forty pounds lighter, having survived still-air temperatures down to minus fifty-one degrees Celsius."
  - [site-snippet] Diary, 23 Dec 2004, "Beyond Dunbar, Alaska": "It's -40°C and bitter"; there was no trail, so he had to find three log cabins 5.5 miles away on a compass bearing.
  - Backpacker: near Dunbar, "trudging along through soft powder in a -40°F wind chill", he "covered only a mile" in a three-day stretch. — https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon
  - Dunbar is on the Parks Highway / Nenana area, south-west of Fairbanks. INFERENCE: his route was Fairbanks, Nenana, Dunbar, Manley, Tanana, then down the Yukon River (Ruby, Galena, Nulato) to Kaltag, then the Kaltag–Unalakleet portage. This is the classic winter trail; only the endpoints are sourced.
- **Yukon River villagers:** Jill Homer recalls "a blog I followed covered some of his interactions with villagers along the Yukon River". She dates her interest to early 2006, as he crossed "the frozen Interior". — https://jilloutside.substack.com/p/the-longest-way-home
- **Meeting Kieffer:**
  - Nexus: during the 2005 Iditarod Trail Invitational (Knik Lake to Nome on foot, from 26 Feb 2005), Kieffer "met with Karl Bushby at the old woman's cabin between Kaltag and Unalakleet". This puts Bushby on the portage around March 2005. — https://nexusexpeditions.blogspot.com/2008/01/expedition-history.html
  - Seattle Times: "Kieffer met Bushby in 2005 when Kieffer was doing the 1,100-mile Iditarod ... by foot." — https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060603/dimitri03m/much-suspense-near-tip-of-the-world
  - **Conflict on Kieffer's time:** 37 days (Nexus) vs 41 days (Seattle Times, 6 Apr 2006). — https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060406/dimitri06m/hes-an-amazing-guy-
  - **Conflict on how they met:** [site-snippet] the site says he met Kieffer "while waiting out a summer". Weaker than the Nexus account.
- **Unalakleet stop:** Wikipedia: he intended to leave Fairbanks and cross the strait in winter 2005 but "made it only as far as Unalakleet before spring began and his U.S. visa expired once again" (*Giant Steps* pp. 312–313). Wikipedia elsewhere loosely calls this an "attempt" at the strait in 2005.
- **Where he spent summer/autumn 2005:** Unknown (GAP). Under his rules he would have flown out on visa expiry and returned to Unalakleet.
- **Media in 2005:** Northwest Cable News video (26 Jan 2005), Nome Nugget (Apr 2005), Fairbanks Daily News-Miner (16 Dec 2005), Yorkshire Post (27 Dec 2005). These are listed in Kieffer's press list; I could not find their text.

## 7. Alaska, part 2: Unalakleet, Nome, Wales (Dec 2005 to 17 Mar 2006)

**Takeaway:** He restarted at Unalakleet in December 2005 and walked the Norton Sound coast (Shaktoolik and beyond) to Nome by late January 2006. With Kieffer he then went Nome, Cape Woolley, Teller, Wales. Off Cape Woolley in early February they drifted 28 miles out on broken ice and were lifted off by an Evergreen helicopter, then continued. They started the strait from Wales on 17 Mar 2006.

- **Norton Sound coast:**
  - [site-snippet] Diary, Friday 30 Dec 2005, "Towards Shaktoolik, Alaska": "I feel like death warmed up... The faint light of Shaktoolik never seems to get closer. Suddenly I'm yanked off my feet and dragged across the snow — my sled has gone over the sea wall in the dark."
  - [site-snippet] "Before Nome, Karl survived savage coastal blizzards and a midnight fall through the unstable sea ice of Norton Sound."
- **Nome arrival:**
  - Matt Butcher, a Nome teacher, wrote on Thursday 26 Jan 2006 that Bushby "finally got to Nome a few days ago". Butcher met him in the teachers' lounge while Bushby waited to talk to the high-school journalism class. Butcher quotes: "He's my age, 33" (an error: Bushby was 36) and "Apparently, no one has ever done it from this side." — https://mjb0123.blogspot.com/2006/01/
- **Nome to Wales:**
  - Nexus: "Feb 2006 Nome to Wales, Alaska, 185 km (115 miles), 9 days", together with Bushby.
  - [site-snippet] Diary: 2 Feb 2006 at Cape Woolley; 5 Feb 2006 "On the drifting ice" ("we're twenty-eight miles out to sea"); 18 Feb 2006 "Beyond Teller, Alaska".
  - INFERENCE: the "9 days" counts walking days only, because the dated entries span at least 2–18 Feb.
- **Ice drift and helicopter:**
  - [site-snippet] "In one storm he and Dimitri were driven thirty kilometres out to sea on floating ice and had to be lifted off by helicopter... within the hour our friends from Evergreen Helicopters put down and pluck us off the ice."
  - Seattle Times, 6 Apr 2006: they slept on an ice block that broke loose, "drifted 28 miles out to sea", were "rescued by helicopter", and Kieffer got frostbite on a finger.
  - **Conflict on timing:** Seattle Times, 3 Jun 2006 places the drift (36 hours) during the strait trek and has Kieffer treated in Nome.
  - The site's diary dates (2–5 Feb, near Cape Woolley) put the drift on the Nome–Wales leg, before 17 Mar. This resolves the "unbroken path" worry in `verify_bering_russia.md`: the lift-off came before the strait, and they resumed from the coast.
  - Where exactly they resumed after the lift-off is still unverified.
- **Departure:** 17 Mar 2006 from Wales, per AP. The Seattle Times, 3 Jun 2006 says "left Nome on March 17", which is probably an error. Kieffer described it as the "cutoff date".
- **Supply plan:** Food for 15 days, plus drops near the Russian border (Seattle Times, 3 Jun 2006). They were resupplied once by plane and once by helicopter (Filson). — https://www.filson.com/blogs/journal/profiles-the-bering-strait-crossing
