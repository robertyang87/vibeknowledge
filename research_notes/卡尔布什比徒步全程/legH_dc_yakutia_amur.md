## 1. The 2013–14 protest walk (new details)

### Start, project, team
- **Start date: 3 September 2013 from Los Angeles** (LVW: "He left LA on September 3, traveling about 20 miles a day"). https://lasvegasweekly.com/as-we-see-it/2013/oct/25/intrepid-globetrotter-karl-bushby/
- **Correction:** this settles the open "start date" question in verify_bering_russia.md §4.
  - The Otello/Holod-family claim "весной 2014-го Бушби отправился в новый поход" (Otello) is **wrong** for the start.
  - Wikipedia's "protested in 2013" is right only for the start.
  - Outside's "ensuing 12-month journey" fits Sep 2013 to Sep 2014.
- **Start point:** Tappis says the only crew shoots were "a couple of days in DC and LA, going to the Russian embassy, and shooting him leaving the pier in LA". **[INFERENCE]** The pier is probably the Santa Monica pier, but Tappis does not name it. https://www.motionpictures.org/2015/05/karl-bushby-attempts-36000-mile-trek-in-the-walk-around-the-world/
- **Project name and team:**
  - The project was called **"The 3000"** (LVW).
  - Producers: Jordan Tappis and Beau Willimon of Westward Productions.
  - **Zach(ary) Guglin**, producer, ran ground support and was Bushby's main contact.
  - **Chris Amos** was the editor (MPA).
  - Willimon first met Bushby in 2010 for *Malibu* magazine (WaPo). Tappis says Willimon called him "in 2011 from a bookstore in London".
  - Early 2011: the pair paid **~$30,000** to send Bushby from Melaque to Chukotka with a camera (Backpacker 2012 profile).
- **How it was filmed:**
  - Almost entirely self-filmed: an "arsenal of cameras", daily confessionals, every phone call recorded, and a shot list.
  - Media cards were posted to the editors every other week.
  - Adam took over the camera while he was there (MPA).
  - Bushby also had to make social posts, a 12-minute "sizzler", and detours for footage (LVW).
- **Bushby's reaction to the idea:** "When they first came up with the idea I almost laughed myself off the chair. Why would I want to go 3,000 miles in the wrong direction?" (LVW). Also: "a kind of vomit reflex" (Outside).

### Route as documented (states in order)
- California:

  - Mojave / Death Valley area (hockey-stick incident)

- Arizona/Nevada border: Hoover Dam (planned)

- Texas Panhandle: blizzard (WaPo)

- North Carolina: Troy, Biscoe, Robbins, Carthage on NC-24, July 2014
- Virginia: Richmond area, about 1 Sep 2014 (snippet)
- Washington DC
- **[INFERENCE]** Between Colorado/Texas and Georgia the line probably ran through Oklahoma/Arkansas or Tennessee or Mississippi/Alabama. **No source found**, so do not state these states. NM/AZ are geographically likely between Nevada and the Texas Panhandle but are unconfirmed.

### Son Adam (from WaPo 14 Aug 2014, Bill Donahue)
https://www.washingtonpost.com/lifestyle/magazine/will-russia-thwart-karl-bushbys-epic-hike-around-the-globe/2014/08/14/6c6d120a-0c59-11e4-8c9a-923ecc0c7d23_story.html (full text via https://www.newssniffer.co.uk/articles/824040/versions/2405039)
- **Who he was:**
  - "lanky 24-year-old son, Adam, who's visiting for two weeks from his home in Belfast"; Willimon paid his flight.
  - Karl had seen Adam "only twice since 1998".
  - Adam's daughter **Harley was born in April 2014**, so Karl was already a grandfather.
- **His hip:**
  - He had an undiagnosed hip problem after a 2011 motorcycle crash ("dead for 10 minutes").
  - The pair waited three days in a hotel for the pain to ease.
  - On the first day Adam was done after 6 miles, at the western fringe of Biscoe.
  - The next day: 8 hours at Subway. On the third day Adam stayed in Biscoe while Karl and Donahue walked 14 miles east to Robbins. Karl then hitched a ride into Carthage.
  - **Note:** this is a non-walking lift, between filming days, from a point he had reached on foot. It is not covered by other sources.
- **The Beast in the US:** a **180-pound cart** ("The Beast").
- **Vokrug Sveta** says Adam first saw his father again at 14, in Alaska (≈2004).
- **Conflict:** WaPo/LVW say Karl had seen his family/son "only twice since 1998". Vokrug Sveta says Adam first saw him again at 14, in Alaska. Both can be true.

### Embassy, outcome and people
- **Embassy contacts:**
  - The Russian Embassy spokesman was **Yevgeniy Khorishko**. He gave one-sentence replies and told WaPo: "We do not give any comments on visa issues."
  - Guglin: "He's walking 3,500 miles to get a 15-minute meeting."
- **Expert pessimism:** Donald Jensen (Johns Hopkins CTR) said "about zero". John Lough (Chatham House) said "on a blacklist for a number of years".
- **New detail on the cause of the ban (WaPo):**
  - Russia had granted him **four visas between 2007 and 2011**.
  - In 2011 he "lost his wallet in a remote Russian village, then, after he traveled two hours to the nearest cash machine, he inadvertently wandered into one of Far Eastern Russia's many off-limits, militarized security zones".
  - In 2006 they had seized his laptop and found photos with US soldiers in Alaska, which raised spy suspicions.
  - **[INFERENCE]** This fits the 26 Mar 2011 Chersky/Nizhnekolymsky protocol in seg3 §4. The "two hours to the cash machine" suggests he travelled by vehicle to Chersky, which would be an off-route trip.
- **Arrival:**
  - WaPo (mid-July reporting) projected "just after Labor Day" (1 Sep 2014).
  - IOL 1 Sep 2014 places him in the Richmond area (snippet).
  - Holod: «Конечной точкой стало российское посольство, где он лично передал обращение».
  - Bushby in the Daily Beast (snippet, from an earlier session): once the story ran, the embassy invited him to re-submit when he arrived, "only weeks outside D.C.".
  - **Exact arrival date: NOT FOUND.**
  - **[INFERENCE]** He arrived in early to mid September 2014, and the visa followed on 23 Oct 2014.
- **Distance claims:**

  | Distance | Source |
  |---|---|
  | 3,000 mi | LVW, the "The 3000" project name |
  | 3,500 mi | Guglin (WaPo), AP |
  | 3,600 mi | Outside, MPA, Backpacker |
  | 4,800 km | Holod |

- **Ru-Wikipedia** says "в Вашингтон **и обратно**" (and back), with no source and an [нет АИ] tag. **Dubious.** No other source has a return walk.
- **Documentary:** *The Walk Around the World*, Nat Geo, aired **Fri 15 May 2015, 9/8c**, directed by Jordan Tappis (MPA). Adam "couldn't do it for more than a few days" (Tappis).

## 2. Russia 2015: restart and the tundra (new details and a conflict)

- **ykt.ru, 18 Mar 2015, by Elena Alekseeva** (confirmed through the ru-Wikipedia footnote).
  - Content cited by ru-Wikipedia: about $800 at the start; the Darién as the hardest stage besides Bering; and that he was filming a Nat Geo documentary.
  - The page itself still redirects.
  - This confirms he was **in Yakutia by mid-March 2015**.
- **CONFLICT: where was he on 15 May 2015?**
  - Outside (15 May 2015, full text): "**Currently in Berlin**, Bushby has resumed his global trek, spending the last two and a half months walking across Russia … he is currently looking for a summer route through Siberia. 'Failing that, we'll probably end up going back next spring.' **By the end of his next leg, Bushby hopes to reach the Road of Bones**."
    https://www.outsideonline.com/culture/books-media/karl-bushby-questions-his-decision-walk-around-world/
  - Seg3's "Yakutsk by 15 May 2015" came from the Daily Beast snippet ("in Yakutsk … headed southwest") and an older Wikipedia line. The current en-Wikipedia has dropped that line.
  - **Resolution [INFERENCE]:**
    - The spring 2015 90-day stint (~early March to mid May) ended **before** the Road of Bones, somewhere on the tundra/winter road towards Sasyr.
    - He then flew out, possibly via Yakutsk, which would explain a Yakutsk dateline, and was in Berlin by mid May.
    - "Yakutsk by 15 May 2015 on foot" is therefore **probably wrong**. It is ~1,000+ km from Sasyr via Ust-Nera and Khandyga, and Outside says the Road of Bones had not yet been reached.
- **Sasyr → Road of Bones:** the X post (Nov 2015) says "2015". **[INFERENCE]** This was either the end of the spring 2015 leg or an autumn/winter 2015–16 leg. Reaching the R504 near Ust-Nera, then Tompon/Khandyga, then Yakutsk most likely falls in winter 2015–16.
- **Tompon-district school visit:** the snippet names "Сасыльская школа"? **Uncertain spelling.** Sasyr itself is in Momsky district, not Tompon. The outlet's title says Tompon district.
  - **[INFERENCE]** The school may be in a Tompon village on the R504 (e.g. Teply Klyuch, Kyubeme, Khandyga), or the snippet garbled "Сасырская".
- **Off-season location:** Outside puts him in **Berlin** in May 2015. This is new. Previously only Mexico, US and Canada were known for the off-seasons.
  - Tappis (2015): between tundra windows he "would go back to Melaque because it was cheap".
  - Otello: he flies to Mexico every few months to rest.

## 3. 2016: Aldan → Neryungri → Amur/Zabaykalye (new details)
- **Gazeta-bam full text** (adds to seg3):
  - Rescuer Fabrin saw videos of the Bering crossing and of him «как вплавь пересекает Алдан». **[INFERENCE]** The swim was probably where the A360 crosses the Aldan near Tommot. Swimming rather than using a ferry or bridge fits his "no transport" rule, but no source says where he swam or whether a ferry or bridge was available.
  - His stated goal in June 2016 was **"Монголия и Иран"**, which shows the Mongolia route was already planned in 2016.
  - The original issue is gazeta-bam **no. 23 of 08.06.2016**, citing IA Port Amur.
- **Port Amur 5 Dec 2016 (original text)**
  https://portamur.ru/news/detail/na-amurskiy-sever-prishel-krugosvetnyiy-puteshestvennik-karl-bushbi/
  - Headline: "Британец спустя 17 лет скитаний добрался в Ерофей Павлович".
  - The geotag shows him "в поселке Ерофей Павлович Сковородинского района **либо на подходе к нему**". The local administration had heard nothing.
  - "Британец планировал идти в Тынду, но затем долгое время о нем ничего не было слышно. Судя по соцсетям, **после Якутии** Карл Бушби побывал в Забайкальском крае — в его профиле есть фото из города Могочи."
  - **CONFLICT / ORDER:** walking west, the order is Tynda → Skovorodino area → **Erofey Pavlovich → Mogocha**.
    - Port Amur implies Mogocha came first, which is geographically backwards for a westbound walker.
    - **[INFERENCE]** The 5 Dec Erofey Pavlovich post was probably a delayed or retrospective post, typical of his social feeds. By late 2016 he had probably reached at least Mogocha. This is unconfirmed; there are no dated Mogocha sources.
  - **Conditions [INFERENCE]:** December on the Amur highway is about −30 to −40 °C. If he was walking then, it was a winter stint pushing or dragging the cart. No source describes it.

## 4. 2017: border and Ulaanbaatar
- **Hull Live / Hull Daily Mail, 8 Aug 2017 (Kevin Shoesmith)**, full text:
  https://www.hulldailymail.co.uk/news/hull-east-yorkshire-news/hull-adventurer-karl-bushby-camels-295155
  - "Since crossing the Russia-Mongolia border **earlier this year**" (no date, no post named).
  - He was in Ulaanbaatar preparing.
  - The camel idea came from "a group of six or seven women" who run desert excursions. Father Keith: "nothing stops him from loading his kit onto camels and tabbing alongside".
  - "Will not have to cross into China, as previously planned" (he later did; see the Asia notes).
  - The Mongolian crossing was expected to take "three years or more".
  - Filming was for **Westward Media**.
- **Crossing post: still UNKNOWN.** Port Amur and gazeta-bam say nothing of Chita, Ulan-Ude or Kyakhta, and searches of Buryat media returned nothing.
  - **[INFERENCE]** Kyakhta–Altanbulag (road to Ulaanbaatar, ~350 km) remains the likeliest. The alternatives on his line from Mogocha via Chita are Solovyevsk–Ereentsav (Zabaykalsky, eastern Mongolia) and Mondy (too far west).
  - From Mogocha via Chita to Kyakhta is ≈1,200–1,400 km. At ~30 km/day that is ≈45 days, which fits a spring 2017 stint ending in Ulaanbaatar before August.
  - **Verdict: unverified.**
