## 2. Panama (Feb–Mar 2001): detention, release, start of the road walk

- **Boca de Cupe, 16 Feb 2001** [site-snippet]: questioned and held overnight for lacking a visa.
- **La Palma, 23 Feb 2001** [site-snippet]: "searched", "processed", and placed in a compound with other detainees. La Palma is the Darién provincial capital, on the Gulf of San Miguel.
- **Helicopter** (TVN-2, quoting Keith Bushby):
  - "En Panamá, por ejemplo, Bushby fue arrestado, trasladado en helicóptero y encarcelado."
  - "Cuando lo soltaron, tuvo que regresar al lugar donde lo recogieron para poder caminar la distancia de nuevo."
- **Release** [site-snippet]: the ordeal ended at the British Embassy, Panama City. A separate site passage says he was released "once the authorities understood who he was and what he was doing". **CONFLICT/ambiguity:** release on the authorities' own initiative versus through the embassy.
  - A search-engine summary claimed Wikipedia says "after negotiations with the British Embassy he was released and allowed to walk under police escort". **I checked Wikipedia: that sentence refers to China, not Panama.** Treat it as a summarizer error.
- **Location CONFLICT, now three-way:**
  - Metetí (Wikipedia, citing *Giant Steps*)
  - Boca de Cupe (arrest, per the site)
  - La Palma (detention compound, 23 Feb, per the site)
  - INFERENCE: the sequence ran Boca de Cupe (picked up) → La Palma (processed; possibly reached by helicopter or boat) → Metetí (jail) → Panama City (embassy, release). The jail term was 18 days, so release falls around early-to-mid March 2001.
- **Re-walk** (INFERENCE from Keith's quote): after release he returned to the Darién pick-up point (Boca de Cupe area) and walked out to the Pan-American Highway, past Yaviza and Metetí, then on to Panama City. So the true "Panama City" start of Leg D is about **mid/late March 2001** (INFERENCE).

## 3. Costa Rica (≈ Apr–May 2001)

- Nothing dated beyond the Backpacker "Costa Rica" photo already noted. I found no La Nación (CR) 2001 article; the only nacion.com hit is the Nov 2026 homecoming wire story.
- INFERENCE: the border crossing was Paso Canoas (Panama → CR), then the Interamericana Sur through Ciudad Neily, Palmar and San Isidro (or the Costanera), then San José or Liberia, then **Peñas Blancas** into Nicaragua. That is the only paved through-route.
- Timing INFERENCE: he was in Nicaragua by 4 Jun 2001 (La Prensa). From Panama City (about mid-March) to Peñas Blancas is about 1,000 km, which at 20–30 km/day plus rest fits **April–May 2001**.

## 4. Nicaragua (≈ late May–Jun/Jul 2001): La Prensa coverage and Beast 2 in León

- **Press:** La Prensa, 4 Jun 2001, section *Nacionales*. Snippet content (paraphrase):
  - He left England 1 Nov 1998 for Chile, starting from Punta Arenas.
  - He crossed South America to Panama in 2 years 7 months.
  - He is a 32-year-old ex-soldier wanting to show a person can cover huge distances on foot without transport.
  - "Karl Bushby pasó por Nicaragua en su caminata que durará doce años."
- **Age CONFLICT:** Bushby was born 30 Oct 1969, so he was **31** in June 2001. La Prensa's "32" is off by one; the 2026 La Prensa piece says 29 at departure, which is correct.
- **Beast 2:**
  - [site-snippet] He welded it himself in a León technical-school workshop.
  - Nicaraguan 2026 press (AFP-derived) says it was made in Nicaragua "en el año 2001" as an "urgent replacement" after irreparable damage to the first cart.
  - Nickname "Wilson" (100noticias and Nueva Ya).
  - Contents: tent, sleeping bags, food, water, clothes (Despacho 505).
- **Builder CONFLICT:** "he welded" (site) versus "custom built for him in Nicaragua" (Getty 2014 caption, which calls it "The Best Mark II"; seen in a search snippet only: https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/news-photo/karl-bushby-keeps-all-of-his-belongings-in-a-cart-called-news-photo/453671642). INFERENCE: he did the work himself with the school's tools and staff help.
- **The school is still unnamed (GAP).** INFERENCE (unverified): candidates in León include an INATEC centre or the UNAN-León workshops.
- **Original-cart CONFLICT:**
  - Despacho 505: the first cart was destroyed "after the first 8,000 km" by southern South America's climate.
  - This conflicts with it reaching Nicaragua at all. He left the cart behind for the Darién (backpack), and Punta Arenas to Colombia is far more than 8,000 km.
  - INFERENCE: either the 8,000 km figure is wrong, or the original Beast was rebuilt or replaced earlier and its last version failed in Panama/Costa Rica. Treat Despacho 505 as weak.
- **Lifespan CONFLICT:** Nueva Ya says the cart lasted "25 años", which fits 2001→2026. Other wording says "más de dos décadas". These are consistent with 2001.
- Route INFERENCE: Peñas Blancas, Rivas, Granada/Masaya, Managua, then **León** (a detour west on the old Pan-Am/León road), then Chinandega and the El Guasaule border into Honduras. León sits on the Chinandega–Guasaule line, which fits a cart rebuild just before leaving Nicaragua.

## 5. Honduras and El Salvador (≈ Jul 2001)

- Nothing dated was found in El Heraldo, La Prensa (HN) or La Prensa Gráfica.
- INFERENCE: El Guasaule, then Choluteca, then San Marcos de Colón or El Amatillo. The Honduran Pacific strip on CA-1 is only about 130 km (2–5 days).
- El Salvador: El Amatillo, then San Miguel, then CA-1/CA-2 to San Salvador. The previous file's "El Salvador 2001" photo caption still rests on an unverified mirror.
- Weather INFERENCE: this is the rainy season (May–Oct) on the Pacific lowlands, hot at about 30–35 °C, with afternoon storms.

## 6. Guatemala (≈ Aug 2001) and the border into Mexico

- No dated source was found. INFERENCE: San Cristóbal Frontera or Las Chinamas / Pedro de Alvarado (from El Salvador), then Escuintla on CA-2 (Pacific), then Retalhuleu and Coatepeque, then **Tecún Umán – Ciudad Hidalgo** or El Carmen – Talismán into Chiapas.
- CA-2 (the coastal highway) leads straight to Tapachula and then Hwy 200 to Tonalá. This is consistent with the Tonalá entry. The highland CA-1 via Guatemala City and La Mesilla would put him on Hwy 190 via Comitán and Tuxtla, missing Tonalá. **So the Tonalá entry favours the coastal CA-2 / Hwy 200 line** (INFERENCE, fairly strong).
- Entry into Mexico INFERENCE: late Aug or early Sep 2001. Tapachula to Tonalá is about 220 km.

## 7. Mexico (≈ late Aug 2001 to 29 Apr 2002)

- **Tonalá, Chiapas, Tue 11 Sep 2001** [site-snippet]: exhausted, he "collapses into a restaurant", sees the 9/11 news on TV and is close to tears.
- **Pacific route:**
  - Search-snippet caption: "Pacific Coast of Mexico in 2002".
  - "Mexico Nogales 2002" photo (Dave's Travel Corner).
  - INFERENCE for the route: Hwy 200 (Tonalá, then Juchitán/Isthmus of Tehuantepec, Salina Cruz, Puerto Escondido, Pinotepa, Acapulco, Zihuatanejo, Lázaro Cárdenas, Manzanillo, Puerto Vallarta), then Tepic, then Hwy 15 (Mazatlán, Culiacán, Los Mochis, Navojoa, Ciudad Obregón, Guaymas, **Hermosillo**, Santa Ana, Ímuris), then Nogales.
  - No intermediate Mexican town is sourced (GAP).
  - **Weather (INFERENCE):** the Isthmus of Tehuantepec in autumn means strong "Tehuano" north winds. Pacific coast heat and humidity. The Sonoran Desert in Feb–Apr 2002 means mild days and cold nights, warming by late April.
- **Duration check (INFERENCE):** Tonalá to Nogales by the Hwy 200 / Hwy 15 line is about 3,300–3,600 km. At about 25 km/day plus rest days, mid-Sep 2001 to late Apr 2002 (about 7.5 months) fits **without** any long break. The previous file's "seven months" stands.
- **Exit:** 29 Apr 2002 at Nogales (Arizona Daily Star, already noted). **CONFLICT:** the site says "May 2002". No new evidence; keep 29 Apr.
- **Gunpoint** (see table #10): this is the only "incident" attributed to Mexico in any source, and it is undated. Do NOT assign it to Leg D without the memoir.
- **Visa note** (INFERENCE): Mexico gave 180-day FMT tourist cards in 2001. Entry around late Aug/early Sep 2001 plus 180 days runs out around late Feb/early Mar 2002, before 29 Apr. So he probably needed an extension or renewal in Mexico (unverified). This would make a good question for *Giant Steps* chapter 7.
