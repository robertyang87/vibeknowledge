## 1. Who is Karl Bushby? Background and motivation

### Takeaway
Bushby was born in Hull on 30 March 1969. He served about 11–12 years as a British Army paratrooper (3rd Battalion, Parachute Regiment) and left around 1996. Aged 29, he set out to walk an unbroken path from the southern tip of South America home to Hull. Sources say he did it partly to prove himself and partly to escape a painful divorce.

### Cited Findings
- **Birth and childhood:** Born 30 March 1969 in Hull, Yorkshire, and raised in the Sutton Park area. He was diagnosed with dyslexia at 13. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
- **Army service:** He joined the British Army at 16 and served 11 years with the 3rd Battalion, Parachute Regiment. The infobox gives 1985–1996, rank Corporal. He served in Northern Ireland (Operation Banner), married Angela from Belfast, had a son, Adam, and divorced in 1995. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
  - **Conflicting figures:** Backpacker says he served 12 years, needed five attempts at the entrance test, and joined because his father, a former star paratrooper, encouraged him. — [Backpacker, "The Long Way Home"](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - **Conflicting figures:** Backpacker places a "nasty divorce" around 1998, which conflicts with Wikipedia's 1995. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
- **Motivation:** He wanted to prove himself to his former paratrooper peers and to escape the aftermath of his divorce. He left behind an eight-year-old son, Adam. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
- **The bet story:** A 2025 profile says that in 1998, aged 29, he bet friends he could walk from the southern tip of South America to England. — [Blick](https://www.blick.ch/ausland/da-bin-ich-an-meine-grenzen-gestossen-brite-laeuft-seit-27-jahren-um-die-welt-jetzt-will-er-nach-hause-id21527064.html)
  - This "drunken bet" framing appears mainly in recent popular coverage, e.g. [Boxlife Magazine](https://boxlifemagazine.com/drunken-bet-argentina-to-england-odyssey/). It is not in the 2006 wire reports.
- **Age in 2006:** Wire reports in April 2006 gave his age as 37. — [AP via Spokesman-Review](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/); [Reuters via NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna12315893)
- **Hometown paper:** AP notes that his hometown newspaper is the Hull Daily Mail. — [Spokesman-Review/AP](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)
- **Relationship in Colombia:** In Colombia he met Catalina Estrada, whom he called "the only woman who ever mattered to me". She ended the relationship in 2008 after years of failed visa attempts. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - Wikipedia mentions difficulties getting his partner through Canada in 2003. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)

### Inferences
- The motivation is best described as a mix of personal redemption after the divorce, proving himself after his army career, and adventure. The "bet" is a later popular framing that early sources do not confirm.

### Gaps
- I could not reach a Hull Daily Mail article from 1998, so I have no contemporary local account of his departure.
- I found no primary quote from Bushby in 1998 explaining his motivation.

## 2. Rules of the expedition, plan, distances, equipment and funding

### Takeaway
The goal was to walk "with unbroken footsteps" from Punta Arenas to Hull, a planned route of about 36,000 miles (58,000 km) over about 12 years. He set three main rules:
- No transport of any kind to make forward progress.
- No return to the UK until he arrived there on foot.
- After any forced break, resume from the exact spot where he stopped.

He started with a wheeled handcart he called "The Beast". Funding was chronically thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Goal and plan:**
  - Walk "with unbroken footsteps" from the southern tip of South America to Hull without motorised transport. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
  - The plan was 12 years and about 36,000 miles (58,000 km). Delays stretched it to about 28 years. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
- **No-transport rule:** He may not use any transport to advance the expedition, not even self-propelled vehicles such as bicycles or kayaks. — reported in 2025–26 coverage summarised via [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
- **Home-soil rule:**
  - He may not go home until the walk is finished. — [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
  - Backpacker gives a narrower version: he will not set foot on British soil unless he walks there. He said he would skip his parents' funerals rather than break the rule. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
- **Flying is allowed for logistics only:**
  - He has flown to Alaska, Mexico, Colombia and elsewhere for logistics or visas, but always resumes on foot from where he stopped. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon); [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
  - **Example, 2002:** His US visa expired in Las Vegas in July 2002. He flew to Bogotá and resumed in August once a six-month visa was approved. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
  - **Rule framing:** Some recent sources say he must return to the exact GPS coordinate where he stopped. I found this only in secondary or popular sources; see the search summary of [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-is-home-after-28-years-on-the-road/) and others.
- **Three "gaps" in the route:** The plan identified three water or jungle gaps:
  1. The Darién Gap.
  2. The Bering Strait, the most uncertain for timing.
  3. The English Channel, to be crossed through the Channel Tunnel.

  — summarised from search results citing [angusadventures](https://angusadventures.com/?p=1871). Backpacker says he planned to use the Channel Tunnel's emergency service corridor. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
- **Starting money and cart:**
  - He flew to Punta Arenas with $800 and a wheeled cart of gear. He called the loaded cart "The Beast" on the Andean climbs. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - **Conflicting figures:** Other recent coverage says he started with $500. — search summary citing [Stars and Stripes 2025](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/europe/2025-12-06/british-army-veteran-20001754.html)
  - The task brief mentioned the name "Goliath". I found no source that calls the cart "Goliath". Goliath is the name of the expedition.
- **Cart theft, 2003:** On 1 January 2003 his handcart was stolen in Toole County, Montana. It was later found in a quarry. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
- **Gear on the ice:** On the Bering ice he towed sleds of about 200 lb. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
- **Pace:** He averaged about 2,000 miles per year from 1998 to 2006. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
- **Funding:**
  - He relied on strangers for food and shelter, a few modest sponsorships, and his parents' fundraising in England. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - Around 2008 he lost sponsorship and set up camp in Mexico. He resumed walking in 2011. — [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
  - Backpacker says he moved to Melaque, Mexico, around late 2008. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
- **Distance covered by April 2006:** About 17,000 miles. — [Seattle Times/AP](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump)
  - Reuters said he was "about halfway" through the 36,000-mile trek after seven years. — [NBC/Reuters](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna12315893)
  - Expected arrival in Britain was 2009. — [NBC/Reuters](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna12315893); [Spokesman/AP](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)

### Inferences
- **Total distance:**
  - The planned figure of 36,000 miles / 58,000 km (2006 era) was later revised to about 31,000 miles. Backpacker's editor's note reflects this revision.
  - Recent coverage cites about 49,900–58,000 km.
  - The report should flag this inconsistency and not pick one figure.
- **Wording of the rules:** The "exact spot" rule is consistent with his practice, but its precise wording is not well documented in early primary sources.

## 3. Route stages 1998–2005: Chile to Alaska

### Takeaway
- **1998:** He left Hull on 5 October 1998 and started walking from Punta Arenas on 1 November 1998.
- **South America:** He went north through Patagonia, along the Chilean coast and Andes, then through Peru, Ecuador and Colombia.
- **Darién Gap:** He crossed it in about two months (often given as 2001), then spent 18 days in a Panamanian jail.
- **USA:** He entered at Nogales, Arizona, on 29 April 2002.
- **Canada and Alaska:** He crossed the US and Canada into Alaska by 2005. He fell short of crossing the Bering Strait that winter.

### Cited Findings
- **Departure:** Left Hull on 5 October 1998. Set off from Punta Arenas on 1 November 1998. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby); start date confirmed by [Seattle Times, 15 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060415/deport15m/world-trek-threatened-by-russian-court-order)
- **Patagonia and Chile:**
  - Winds were so strong he walked tilted about 45°, damaging his ankle joints. He ran out of food and hallucinated about food.
  - He crossed long waterless desert stretches in northern Chile.
  - He woke in an ice-covered tent in the Andes.
  - He climbed more than 6,000 ft in three hours on a mountain road.

  — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
- **Route list:** Wikipedia lists Argentina, Peru, Ecuador and Colombia as countries on the South American leg. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
- **Peru:** Recent coverage mentions a mob attack in Peru, a wrist injury he stitched himself, and a stomach infection nursed by a doctor he met there. — search-result summaries of [Daily News SA 2025](https://dailynews.co.za/travel/world/2025-08-01-walking-the-world-karl-bushbys-27-year-journey-heads-for-the-finish-line/) and others. Not verified in full text.
- **Darién Gap (Colombia to Panama):**
  - He crossed a 320 km jungle stretch in two months and was detained for 18 days in Panama before being allowed to continue. — [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
  - He went against the advice of the Colombian military. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - He evaded drug traffickers, arms dealers and FARC guerrillas, including four days swimming down a river in camouflage. — [Seattle Times/AP, 5 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump)
  - **Arrival and jail:** He entered Panama in February 2001. He spent 18 days in jail in Metetí for entering without a visa. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
  - **Conflicting figures:** Other sources date the crossing to 2000 or 2000–01. One account describes the two months as Medellín to Panama City. — search summaries ([angusadventures](https://angusadventures.com/?p=1871); [siakapkeli](https://skreact.siakapkeli.my/articles/27-tahun-keliling-dunia-lelaki-dedah-dua-lokasi-paling-menyeramkan))
  - **Jail reason:** Some reports attribute the jailing to crossing a restricted border zone rather than to a missing visa.
- **Central America and Mexico:**
  - Backpacker has photos from Costa Rica and Paya (Panama) but gives few details. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - He crossed Central America and entered the US in 2002. — [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
- **USA:**
  - Entered at Nogales, Arizona, on 29 April 2002. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
  - His 90-day visa expired in Las Vegas in July 2002. He flew to Bogotá and resumed in August with a six-month visa. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
  - He walked through intense heat in the US Southwest. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - His cart was stolen on 1 January 2003 in Toole County, Montana, and later recovered from a quarry. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
- **Canada:** He crossed in 2003. The partner visa problems date from this period. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
- **Alaska:**
  - He reached Alaska in 2005. — [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
  - Near Dunbar, Alaska, he covered only one mile in three days in a −40°F wind chill. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - He aimed to leave Fairbanks and cross the strait in winter 2005. He reached only Unalakleet before spring and his visa expiry, so the crossing slipped to 2006. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)

### Inferences
- The rough chronology:
  - 1998–2000: South America.
  - Late 2000 to early 2001: Darién Gap.
  - 2001–02: Central America and Mexico.
  - 2002–03: USA.
  - 2003–04: Canada.
  - 2004–05: Alaska.
- Exact border-crossing dates are documented only for:
  - Start: 1 Nov 1998
  - Panama: Feb 2001
  - Nogales: 29 Apr 2002
  - Montana: 1 Jan 2003
  - Bering Strait: Mar 2006

### Gaps
- I found no reliable dates for crossing into Peru, Ecuador, Colombia, Mexico, Canada or Alaska.
- The exact Darién start date and the swamp details are unknown.
- The Metetí jail location comes from Wikipedia only and was not confirmed elsewhere. One search noted Metetí appears in an unrelated case, so treat it with caution.

## 4. The Bering Strait crossing with Dimitri Kieffer (March–April 2006)

### Takeaway
Bushby and Kieffer, a French-American endurance adventurer and ex-Microsoft employee from Seattle, left Wales, Alaska, on 17 March 2006. Wind-driven floes pushed them about 52 miles north, so they covered about 150 miles in 14–15 days to cross a strait 53–58 miles wide. They walked, climbed pressure ridges and swam in dry suits, and reached the Chukotka coast near Uelen about 31 March.

### Cited Findings
- **Departure:**
  - They set off from the Alaskan town of Wales on 17 March 2006. — [AP via Spokesman-Review, 5 Apr 2006](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)
  - Wikipedia gives the crossing as 17–31 March 2006. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
  - **Conflicting figures:** A June 2006 Seattle Times article about Kieffer says they "left Nome" on 17 March. Wales, at Cape Prince of Wales, is the logical jump-off point, and AP names Wales. — [Seattle Times, 3 Jun 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060603/dimitri03m/much-suspense-near-tip-of-the-world)
- **Duration:**
  - 14 days. — [BBC summary](https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~karchung/BBC_Bushby.htm); [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon); [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
  - 15 days. — [Seattle Times/AP, 15 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060415/deport15m/world-trek-threatened-by-russian-court-order)
- **Width of the strait:** Reported figures differ:
  - 56 miles. — [Seattle Times/AP](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump)
  - 53 miles. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - 58 miles (93 km). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby)
- **Distance actually covered:**
  - About 150 miles (240 km). — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby); [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
  - A support member estimated more than 150 miles because shifting ice forced them to backtrack. — [Seattle Times, 6 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060406/dimitri06m/hes-an-amazing-guy)
  - A search summary reported Kieffer saying "200 circuitous miles". Not verified.
- **Conditions:**
  - They towed sleds through broken ice.
  - They climbed pressure ridges about 30 ft high.
  - They swam through 32°F saltwater pools of slush.
  - The wind drifted the floes about 52 miles north while they were trying to head west.
  - Near the end they discarded excess gear, including shotguns and a radio.

  — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - Temperatures were −30°C, walking on "shifting plates of ice". — [BBC (summary)](https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~karchung/BBC_Bushby.htm)
  - Bushby described the ice as a "huge mass of crushed ice and seawater". — [Blick](https://www.blick.ch/ausland/da-bin-ich-an-meine-grenzen-gestossen-brite-laeuft-seit-27-jahren-um-die-welt-jetzt-will-er-nach-hause-id21527064.html)
  - They made brief swims in protective suits. — [Spokesman/AP](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)
  - Kieffer said they swam for about five days through slushy ice in dry suits. — [Seattle Times, 3 Jun 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060603/dimitri03m/much-suspense-near-tip-of-the-world)
- **Disputed drift and helicopter episode:**
  - Seattle Times reported that their tent on an ice block broke loose, they drifted 28 miles out to sea, and a helicopter rescued them. — [Seattle Times, 6 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060406/dimitri06m/hes-an-amazing-guy)
  - The 3 June article says they drifted for 36 hours and were rescued by a helicopter called by satellite phone. — [Seattle Times, 3 Jun 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060603/dimitri03m/much-suspense-near-tip-of-the-world)
  - **Flag:** This conflicts with the "unbroken" claim and with Backpacker's account, which mentions a 52-mile drift but no rescue. It is unclear whether the helicopter episode belongs to this crossing or to an earlier attempt.
- **Frostbite:** Kieffer got frostbite on a finger. — [Seattle Times, 6 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060406/dimitri06m/hes-an-amazing-guy)
- **Who Kieffer was:**
  - An endurance racer living in Seattle, with US and French citizenship. — [Seattle Times/AP](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump)
  - A former Microsoft employee turned adventure traveller. — [Seattle Times, 15 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060415/deport15m/world-trek-threatened-by-russian-court-order)
  - An AP photo caption lists him as "Dimitry", from Anchorage. — [Spokesman/AP](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)
  - He joined only for the strait crossing. — [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)
  - Wikipedia and ExplorersWeb call him a "French adventurer". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby); [ExplorersWeb](https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/)
- **Landing:**
  - They reached Russia's north-eastern tip on a Friday, per Keith Bushby. That Friday was 31 March 2006. — [Spokesman/AP](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)
  - They were detained after entering the settlement of Uelen, near where the Bering Sea meets the Chukchi Sea. — [Spokesman/AP](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)

### Inferences
- **Dates:** The most consistent timeline is departure from Wales on 17 Mar 2006, landfall near Uelen on 31 Mar 2006, and detention on 1 Apr 2006.
- **Duration:** "14 vs 15 days" depends on whether the counting is inclusive.
- **Distance:** Most sources cite a straight-line width of 53–58 miles against about 150 miles walked.

### Gaps
- I found no exact landing point, such as Cape Dezhnev, in fetched sources.
- I could not reconcile the helicopter-rescue claim with the "unbroken path" claim. A primary account from Bushby or his blog is needed.

## 5. Arrest in Russia, trial, fine and appeal (April–May 2006); aftermath to 2008

### Takeaway
- **1 April 2006:** FSB border guards detained both men in Chukotka for entering outside an official border crossing. They had business visas but no entry stamps and did not register.
- **14 April 2006:** A court in Lavrentiya fined each man about $72 and ordered deportation, which normally means a five-year ban.
- **5 May 2006:** An appeal court cancelled the expulsion but kept the fine.

Bushby later resumed walking in Chukotka (2007). By late 2008, with funding and visa problems, the expedition stalled in Mexico.

### Cited Findings
- **Detention:**
  - Border guards detained them on 1 April in Chukotka. Both had valid visas but no border-post stamps. — [NBC/Reuters, 14 Apr 2006](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna12315893)
  - The FSB north-eastern border-guard spokesman, Andrei Orlov, said they held Russian business visas but had not done foreigner registration. — [Seattle Times/AP](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump)
  - Authorities noted they carried satellite phones, GPS, a video camera and a pistol. — [Seattle Times/AP](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump)
  - They had planned to walk to Provideniya, which has an airport, to register. They were stopped and held in Lavrentiya, in a hotel under a form of house arrest (Reuters). — [Seattle Times/AP](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump)
  - Russian authorities announced the detention on Tuesday, 4 April. — [Spokesman/AP](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)
  - Kieffer said his visa allowed entry only via Moscow. — [Seattle Times, 3 Jun 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060603/dimitri03m/much-suspense-near-tip-of-the-world)
- **Keith Bushby on the entry problem:** "Because they were walking across the Bering Strait, they could not take the normal route... they didn't have the correct stamps and a landing permit." — [Seattle Times/AP](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060405/strait05/36000-mile-trek-hits-speed-bump)
- **British Embassy:** Spokesman Anton Atrashkin said: "We can confirm that a British national is undergoing checks in Chukotka." — [Spokesman/AP](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2006/apr/05/adventurers-held-by-russians-after-crossing/)
- **Trial, Friday 14 April 2006:**
  - A court in Lavrentiya fined each man $72 and ordered both deported. Deportees are usually barred for five years. — [NBC/Reuters](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna12315893); [Seattle Times, 15 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060415/deport15m/world-trek-threatened-by-russian-court-order)
  - They had 10 days to appeal, and the appeal could take up to four months. — [Seattle Times, 15 Apr 2006](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20060415/deport15m/world-trek-threatened-by-russian-court-order)

- **Appeal, 5 May 2006:**
  - The Chukotka appeal court cancelled the expulsion, ruling there was no ill intent. It kept a fine of about $75 (about R495) each.
  - **Conflicting figures:** The fine is given as $72 or $75, and as about 2,000 roubles in some reports.

### Inferences
- Several sources describe the "deportation" loosely. The best-supported sequence is:
  1. Fine and deportation order on 14 Apr.
  2. Deportation cancelled on appeal on 5 May, with the fine kept.
  3. Both left Russia by air to Nome on about 24 May 2006.
- Bushby later returned to resume from Uelen/Chukotka (2007), in keeping with his rules.
