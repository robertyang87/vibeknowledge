## 2. The cart, money, gear and people (new only)

- **The Beast:**
  - "carro metálico de dos ruedas" (a two-wheeled metal cart) carrying "su carpa, agua, raciones y las pocas mudas de ropa" (his tent, water, rations and his few changes of clothes) (El Serenense; also the Spanish search extract).
  - Bier calls it a "trolley" that he was **pushing**. Other sources say he **pulled** it ("hauled", "harnessed": El Serenense says "enganchado"). This is a minor conflict; he probably did both on different terrain. **Inference.**
  - The tyres were worn out and patched in the Coquimbo area (El Serenense).
  - Magis (ITESO, Mexico) says: "La primera carretilla no llegó muy lejos" ("The first cart did not get far") ([Magis](https://magis.iteso.mx/nota/karl-bushby-hacia-el-horizonte/)).
    - This suggests there was an **earlier cart before "The Beast"**, or that the first design failed early.
    - **Conflict/unclear:** Every other source has The Beast from Punta Arenas until it was retired in northern Peru. The Magis line may refer to "The Beast" itself being replaced in Peru by a donkey. Unresolved.
- **Gear:**
  - A tent and "una cámara de plástico" (a plastic camera); no sponsorship ([La República, 2 Nov 2026](https://larepublica.pe/amp/mundo/2026/11/02/el-britanico-que-volvio-a-casa-tras-28-anos-de-viaje-a-pie-salio-de-chile-en-1998-atraveso-30-paises-y-recorrio-50000-km-hasta-inglaterra-186420)).
  - Paper maps (search extract, AOL/Far & Wide).
- **Money reconciliation:**
  - Angus Adventures says "set off with just £500 ($800 US)" ([angusadventures](https://angusadventures.com/karl-bushby-walking-around-the-world-in-goliath-expedition/)).
  - Magis says he left Hull "con 500 libras en el bolsillo" (with 500 pounds in his pocket).
  - **So the "$500 vs $800" conflict in seg1 is likely a pounds/dollars mix-up: £500 ≈ US$800 at 1998 rates.** **Inference.** The 1999 cash infusion from Bier (US$100) shows how thin the budget was.
- **Resupply plan:** "Food and other basic supplies will be acquired en route" (Angus Adventures, quoting the old expedition text).
- **People named in Leg A:**
  - Michael Bier (Austrian in Chile) and, indirectly, his friend Dieter in Vienna.
  - Bushby's mother Angela later wrote to Bier thanking him (BBC).
  - The Piedra Buena schoolchildren (unnamed).
  - The Coquimbo truckers, bomberos and mechanics (unnamed).
  - Background: his father **Keith** helped plan the route: "juntos trazaron una línea absurdamente larga en un mapa" ("together they drew an absurdly long line on a map") (Magis). La República says the idea came from Keith asking whether one could walk "de Londres a Nueva York por Bering" (from London to New York via the Bering Strait).
- **Postcard coda (beyond Leg A):** Karl said just before Hungary → Austria (2026): "This postcard has been on a few adventures. It's a bit ratty but it'll get to Michael's old neighbour, as promised" (BBC via News Beep). The card's German text, translated: "This card was given to Karl Bushby on 7.6.1999, who is walking on foot from Punta Arenas via Bering Strait to London… I hope it arrives with you and you invite Karl for a few days. Greetings, Michael."

## 3. Conflicts and timing analysis

1. **El Serenense "early months of 1999" at La Serena vs the other anchors:**
   - The official site has Santiago → Peru border (more than 2,000 km) ending ~1 Nov 1999. Backpacker has a winter night in the Andes (June–Aug).
   - La Serena is about 470 km north of Santiago. If he was there in Jan–Mar 1999, he would have reached Santiago latitude within 3–5 months of leaving Punta Arenas (about 2,800–3,000 km via Argentina, i.e. 25–30 km/day with no rest). He would then have taken about 8 months for the remaining ~1,600 km to Arica. That is possible but odd.
   - **More consistent reconstruction (inference):** Andes crossing in about May–June 1999 (the winter tent) → in Chile by 7 Jun 1999 (Bier) → Santiago about June → La Serena about July → Atacama Aug–Oct → Arica by ~1 Nov 1999. I rate El Serenense's dating low.
2. **Where he crossed the Andes:**
   - No source names the pass.
   - "High-desert plateau east of the Andes could not sustain the expedition" (site), plus the 6,000 ft climb in 3 hours (Backpacker), plus "north through Santiago" next, are consistent with **Mendoza → Paso Los Libertadores/Cristo Redentor (≈3,200 m, Ruta 7 → CH-60) → Los Andes → Santiago**. This is the only all-season paved pass near Santiago. **Inference only.**
   - Alternatives: a southern pass (e.g. Pino Hachado or Cardenal Samoré, then Ruta 5 north through the Chilean Central Valley). Unconfirmed.
3. **Argentina route:**
   - Piedra Buena implies **Ruta 3** (Atlantic coast) at least in southern Santa Cruz.
   - **Inference:** Ruta 3 north (Río Gallegos → Piedra Buena → San Julián → Caleta Olivia → Comodoro Rivadavia → Trelew?) and then west, or a switch to Ruta 40 inland. Not established. No Argentine town besides Piedra Buena is attested.
4. **Push vs pull of the cart:** see §2.
5. **Grokipedia** (AI-generated; [link](https://grokipedia.com/page/Karl_Bushby)) has a single uncited claim: three days in the Andes "relying solely on a single banana". **Unverified.** Its "odd jobs" funding claim is also unverified.
6. **Pace:** A search extract (source page not identified; likely AOL/Far & Wide 2025) speaks of "hunger days, living on discarded food and roadside forage at thirty kilometres a day". This is consistent with the ~19 mi/day in seg1. No daily log was found.

## 5. Weather, terrain and state of mind (new only)

- **Weather and terrain:**
  - Punta Arenas start in "los vientos implacables" ("the relentless winds") ([Zona Zero, May 2026](https://zonazero.cl/2026/05/la-increible-historia-de-karl-bushby-salio-caminando-de-punta-arenas-hace-28-anos-hacia-inglaterra/)).
  - "vientos extremos en la Patagonia" ("extreme winds in Patagonia") (La República).
  - The Coquimbo stretch was the "last break before the Atacama" (El Serenense).
- **State of mind:**
  - The *Giant Steps* prologue, as quoted in Spanish by Magis, calls the scheme "Un plan bastante malo, grande y estúpido, lo suficiente como para llamar la atención de todos" ("A pretty bad, big and stupid plan, enough to get everyone's attention").
  - Magis also says "El plan no estaba del todo claro" ("The plan wasn't entirely clear").
  - Goodreads reviewers note the diary records "miles traveled, things he ate and individuals he met who gave him a helping hand" (Janet), and his loneliness (my.bookshelf.87). Neither is specific to Leg A.
  - Goodreads reviewer Chris (Oct 2026) was born in Punta Arenas (trivia).
  - No verbatim Leg-A diary quotes were retrievable.
