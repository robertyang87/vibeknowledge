## 1. Mongolia and the "Steppes to the West" camel team (2017–early 2018)

**Arrival.** Wikipedia says: "Bushby crossed the Mongolia–Russia border in 2017, and on 8 August 2017 he was in Ulaanbaatar." It cites WestBound Horizons and the Hull Daily Mail of 8 Aug 2017 (the HDM headline slug mentions "camels"). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Bushby
- The border crossing point (Kyakhta–Altanbulag is the obvious one) is **not sourced**. [INF only]
- [CONFLICT] Huck (22 Mar 2018) says Bushby met Baigalmaa "Baikal" Norjmaa "in 2016 in the suburbs of Ulaanbaatar, after finishing the Siberian leg". A search snippet of the official site places the Mongolia stage in "2016–2017". It is possible he reached Ulaanbaatar at the end of the 2016 season and that "2017" refers to the expedition year. Unresolved. — https://www.huckmag.com/article/adventure-steppes-west-baigalmaa-norjmaa-trek-mongonlia-london-camel

**The STTW project**
- Montsame (8 Sep 2017) describes a ~12,000 km Ulaanbaatar→London Silk Road caravan led by Baigalmaa Norjmaa, who runs Off Road Mongolia, with 10 Bactrian camels.
  - Named team: Karl Bushby; Americans **Angela Marie Maxwell** and Anna Harrington; German Murat Tengrisson; Mongolian Erdenechimeg Erdenebileg.
  - The start was set for "early September" 2017 from Ulaanbaatar.
  - Stage 1 was Mongolia (training and filming). Stage 2 was "China or Russia, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Iran, Turkey". Stage 3 was the Balkans to the UK.
  - The plan was ~30 km/day, with completion in three years.
  - Westward Productions, the TV company that backs Bushby, supported it.
  - Source: https://montsame.mn/jp/read/131396
- Bushby helped start the project. He encouraged Baikal and helped recruit the team. The sponsor MNB World arranged for the 10 camels to come from the Gobi. Bushby's no-transport rule allowed a camel to carry his gear while he walked beside it. — Huck; ExplorersWeb 16 Apr 2019, https://explorersweb.com/hiking-the-world-updates-from-the-road/
- Before the Gobi he had pulled his belongings on a hand-cart. — ExplorersWeb 2019
- The camels were 4–5 years old, untrained, and struggled with the winter. — ExplorersWeb 2019
- Angela Maxwell (blog, 25 Feb 2018) calls STTW "a meeting of creativity between Karl Bushby… and Baigalmaa Baikal".
  - Angela and Karl recruited a seven-person team.
  - She says "From August to mid-December I gave my all to Steppes to the West."
  - Trainers from Australian Camels (Russell and Tara) were involved.
  - There were disputes over **nose pegs**, and she left "to stay true to her integrity".
  - She writes: "All the team members have left except for Baikal… what hurt us all the most was leaving the camels."
  - Source: https://www.shewalkstheearth.com/the-walk/after-mongolia-and-feet-back-in-europe
- Bushby's YouTube channel (@KarlcBushby, fetched listing) has "The Camels STTW", "Camels Arrive STTW", "First day of Camel training STTW" (each ~9 years ago) and "STTW first days west" (~8 years ago). Together these support camel arrival and training in summer 2017 and departure in late 2017. — https://www.youtube.com/@KarlcBushby/videos

**Start date [CONFLICT]**
- Montsame (planned): early September 2017.
- Huck: "set off in November".
- Instagram post of 16 Oct 2017 showing a team meal (per OnTheGoSolo): consistent with an Oct/Nov departure.
- ExplorersWeb 2019: Bushby "joined… in Ulaanbaatar in late 2017".

**Break-up**
- Huck: seven started. The five international members quit after about 400 km, citing injury, illness, or being unable to cope. Temperatures fell as low as −50 °C, and the team melted snow for water. As of Mar 2018, Baikal's brother (a policeman) and the 10 camels were camped "about 480 km short of the Chinese border".
- ExplorersWeb (8 Mar 2023 and 28 Apr 2025): "After 1,130km, several disagreements fractured the group, and Bushby continued into China by himself." — https://explorersweb.com/long-distance-hiking-roundup-2/ and https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-begins-home-stretch-of-27-year-round-the-world-walk/
- [CONFLICT] 400 km (Huck, about the international members generally) versus 1,130 km (ExplorersWeb, about Bushby). Bushby may have stayed longer than the others. [INF]
- OnTheGoSolo: "by January 2018, Bushby and most of the other members had gone their separate ways." The team broke up in "early 2018" per ExplorersWeb 2019. — https://onthegosolo.com/where-is-karl-bushby-2019/
- Baikal later continued alone. By 2019 she was in Kazakhstan, where camels drew crowds ("almost a zoo attraction"). — ExplorersWeb 2019; interview of 8 Jan 2019, https://explorersweb.com/interview-with-baigalmaa-norjmaa-the-mongolian-camel-lady/

**Gobi crossing.** ExplorersWeb says he trekked "across the Gobi Desert with camels". The exact route and the Mongolian exit post into China are **not sourced**. Because he then went through Xinjiang, the likely exits are western posts such as Takeshiken/Bulgan or Yarant. [INF, unverified]

## 2. Meeting Angela Maxwell

- The standard version is that they "first met while crossing the Gobi Desert… joined for a while, then parted ways". — ExplorersWeb 19 Oct 2024, https://explorersweb.com/karl-bushby-and-angela-maxwell-swim-across-the-caspian-sea/
- [CONFLICT] ExplorersWeb (8 Mar 2023) says Maxwell "first met Bushby in **2014**" and later worked with him in the Gobi. Montsame (Sep 2017) already lists her as a team member at the start of STTW. Taken together, they **knew each other before the Gobi** and worked together on STTW from Aug to Dec 2017.
- Maxwell's own background:
  - She began walking in 2014 and spent 6.5–7 years walking ~32,000 km across 14 countries and 4 continents.
  - She was assaulted in Mongolia on her earlier crossing.
  - After STTW she walked the US from near Washington DC, and also walked Georgia→Turkey.
  - She later helped Bushby plan the swim.
  - Sources: ExplorersWeb 2023, 2019; https://www.shewalkstheearth.com/
- Her site calls Bushby a "friend and colleague". No source describes a romantic relationship.

## 3. China / Xinjiang (2018)

- Wikipedia (citing karlbushby.world/story): after entering China in 2018, "his progress was temporarily restricted by local authorities; following intervention by the British Embassy he was allowed to continue under restrictions and police escort", then into Kazakhstan.
- The official site, via search snippets only:
  - A lone officer "fell in beside him without a word" and walked him to a roadside **tyre-repair shack**.
  - Plainclothes police arrived "in three tactical groups", searched his gear and **seized his footage**.
  - He was held in a **hotel under armed guard**. The verdict, given "across a lobby table": the walk was over, and they would **drive him to the Kazakh border**.
  - Accepting that ride would have broken the no-transport rule permanently.
  - The British Embassy intervened. He was then allowed to walk "under a strictly mandated route, a twenty-four-hour escort, no camping and, later, no internet, all the way to the Kazakh border".
- AP (Oct 2026): "He was trailed by police through China." — https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261031/in-28-years-of-walking-karl-bushby-has-overcome-jungle-desert-and-red-tape-now-hes-nearly-home
- Gaps:
  - **No source gives the entry or exit dates, the entry post, or the Kazakh crossing.** Khorgos is plausible but unsourced. [INF]
  - The route through Xinjiang (for example Urumqi) is unsourced.

## 4. Kazakhstan (late 2018–May 2019): yes, he walked it

- Wikipedia: "He subsequently entered Kazakhstan and continued his journey westward."
- ExplorersWeb 2025: "In 2018, he crossed Kazakhstan…" The year is compressed (see below).
- The red "completed" line on the OnTheGoSolo map is captioned "…until November 2018". This suggests he was into Kazakhstan by about Nov 2018. [INF]
- Bushby's own swim-start post (16 Aug 2024): "After 6hrs we clear immigration, and **5 years after entering Eastern Kazakhstan**." This confirms he entered *eastern* Kazakhstan on foot in about 2018–19. — OnTheGoSolo
- Route:
  - He went via **Almaty**. The presumed route was "after leaving Almaty, Kazakhstan in order to arrive in Uzbekistan" (OnTheGoSolo, Jul 2019).
  - The official site (snippet) says he "met up with **Rika** in Almaty before pressing west".
  - Towns between Almaty and Uzbekistan, such as Taraz and Shymkent, are **not sourced**. The natural road is the M39/A2. [INF]
- He crossed into Uzbekistan in **early June 2019**. — Wikipedia; OnTheGoSolo
- [CONFLICT, minor] Tengrinews/InAktau (15 Aug 2024) quotes Bushby saying he "had not been to Kazakhstan before". This must mean western Kazakhstan / Mangystau, or it is a misquote. — https://en.tengrinews.kz/people/an-american-a-briton-and-azerbaijanis-decided-to-swim-across-263827
- [CONFLICT] A Holod (2025) snippet says he crossed Kazakhstan in 2017, and ExplorersWeb says 2018. The best fit is **late 2018 to May 2019**.

## 5. Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan (Jun–Sep 2019); the Iran wall and expulsion

- Uzbekistan:
  - Entered early June 2019 (above).
  - The official site (snippet) says he and Rika "followed the Silk Road through **Samarkand** and the old trading hub of **Bukhara**, heading toward Turkmenistan".
  - His **three-year relationship with Rika ended** in roughly this period: "the worst stretch of the walk since the financial crisis".
  - AP mentions "three years with a woman from Japan". Rika being that woman is [INF].
  - The YouTube video "Central Asia with Rika" was uploaded about 6 months ago.
- Turkmenistan:
  - "Back in 2019, we were lucky to have been given a **30-day visa**."
  - "You need permanent escorts while in Turkmenistan."
  - As the visa neared expiry, his escorts "hurried him toward **Ashgabat**".
  - "My flag is still planted on the border of Iran."
  - Source: Dave's Travel Corner interview, 9 Nov 2022, https://www.davestravelcorner.com/interviews/karl-bushby-adventurer-and-author/
- [INF] Being hurried toward Ashgabat suggests the stopping point was the Gaudan/Bajgiran post south of Ashgabat, or near it. Unsourced.
- Date at the Iran border [CONFLICT]: his father Keith wrote on 27 Aug 2019 "As of today… on the border of Turkmenistan and Iran". Karl told El Informador (Feb 2020) he arrived on **15 Sep 2019**. — OnTheGoSolo
- **Expulsion** (ExplorersWeb, 5 May 2021, https://explorersweb.com/2021/05/05/where-in-the-world-is-karl-bushby/):
  - "His Turkmen government escorts confiscated his equipment and forced him to leave the country" after the 30-day visa expired.
  - "Most of his equipment sits in a garden near the border."
  - Turkmen visas are hard to get, and the borders were then closed.
- **Why Iran failed** (ExplorersWeb 2021):
  - His British passport.
  - His US sponsors could not fund the Iran segment after US–Iran tensions spiked over the killing of General Soleimani (Jan 2020).
  - Rising costs, and an Iranian contact who vanished.
  - His first visa request (summer 2019) was refused (Keith, 27 Aug 2019).
  - On 10 Mar 2020 he was still pursuing Iranian permission.
- The cost of the later fix: re-entering at Uzbekistan meant "losing almost 700 miles of progress", and the walk through Turkmenistan "will have been for naught". — Dave's Travel Corner; OnTheGoSolo
- [CONFLICT] ExplorersWeb (Apr 2025) dates the Turkmenistan crossing and the Iran failure to 2018. That is wrong; the primary sources give 2019.

## 6. The stall, 2019–2024: Mexico (Melaque), the pandemic and planning

- He flew to **Los Angeles** "to update his sponsors and to figure out the Iran dilemma", then to Mexico because it was cheaper. — ExplorersWeb 2021, 2023
- In Mexico by 12 Oct 2019. Birthday in **Cihuatlán**, Jalisco, 1 Dec 2019. — OnTheGoSolo
- **Melaque** (San Patricio Melaque, Cihuatlán municipality, Jalisco) was his long-time Mexican base. Men's Journal (2013) describes "waiting in Melaque, Mexico, 2008 to present". — https://www.mensjournal.com/travel/karl-bushbys-walk-around-the-world-waiting-in-melaque-mexico-2008-to-present-20130514
- His YouTube channel confirms he was in Melaque in the pandemic years:
  - "Storm Hernan Floods Melaque Mexico 2020" (~5 years ago)
  - "Hurricane Nora August 2021 Melaque Mexico" (~3 years ago)
- What he did there (ExplorersWeb 2021):
  - Waited out COVID quarantine.
  - "Helped with rescue efforts after Tropical Storm **Hernan** flooded towns in southwest Mexico" (late Aug 2020; Melaque flooded to 2 m).
  - Was "starting a science-literacy nonprofit".
  - At that point he had walked 43,493 km.
  - Russia also barred entry over COVID.
- In Nov 2022 he was "in the state of Jalisco", training in a bay "almost 4 km wide": backstroke in a flotation jacket, with short fins. — Dave's Travel Corner
- Pandemic and politics, in order:
  - Jan 2022: a "holding pattern" over "geopolitics, route options and pandemic".
  - 6 May 2022: Russia's invasion of Ukraine makes the region harder.
  - 9 May 2022: after a BBC piece, the Caspian swim becomes "Plan A" for summer 2023 (~160 mi / 257 km).
  - 2022: ExplorersWeb says Kazakhstan reopened, but "civil unrest had made walking through bordering countries dangerous". [INF: the January 2022 Kazakhstan unrest and the July 2022 Karakalpakstan unrest]
  - 9 Jan 2023: website and video introduce Maxwell; he calls the swim "Plan Ludicrous", a "water obstacle crossing".
  - 14 Feb 2023: training video; ~10 km/day, sleeping on the support boat and restarting at the same spot each morning; **dog shock collars** to be used to communicate.
  - Sources: OnTheGoSolo; ExplorersWeb 2023
- Big Issue (14 Aug 2026), Bushby: "During the pandemic everything was closed… Then Russia moving into Ukraine was a major obstacle and I ended up having to swim the Caspian". The swim "took an extra year to work out the logistics and the funding". — https://www.bigissue.com/news/karl-bushby-walk-around-the-world/
- AP: in setbacks he retreated to "the U.S. or Mexico, where living was much cheaper"; "a lengthy setback from the pandemic".

## 7. Restart: Bukhara → Kyzylkum → Caspian coast with Angela Maxwell

- The plan as of Nov 2022: "return to Uzbekistan **in March**", then "1,000 miles north and west to the Kazakh coastline", ready to swim "by August". — Dave's Travel Corner
- ExplorersWeb (8 Mar 2023, future tense): they "plan to fly to Bukhara, Uzbekistan, and walk across the desert to the Caspian Sea… training in Mexico".
- Route and distance:
  - From **Bukhara** through the **Kyzylkum** to the Caspian shore "**near the border with Turkmenistan**". — https://www.shewalkstheearth.com/ ; Monowalker
  - Called a "1,000-mile expedition" (Maxwell's site) or "~1,600 km" (Monowalker, https://monowalker.com/en/women-in-adventure/angela-maxell-a-woman-walks-the-world/?v=0b3b97fa6688).
  - Maxwell pushed a Monowalker trailer named "Athena". Bushby pulled his own two-wheeled trailer.
- **Year [CONFLICT]**
  - Bushby's own YouTube video (oEmbed confirmed, author "Karl Bushby") is titled **"1,000mile Section To The Caspian 2023"**.
  - That fits the March 2023 plan, and the 19 Sep 2023 postponement "a year because the boat safety requirements weren't met in time". The Hull blog likewise says "his first attempt… in 2023 failed due to lack of support from safety officials" (https://www.hikinginlondon.co.uk/blog-hull-man-swims-300km-across-caspian-sea).
  - Monowalker and Maxwell's site say "in **2024**". ExplorersWeb gives no year, and its Apr 2025 text wrongly puts the swim in "October 2024".
  - **My best reading [INF]:** the Bukhara→Caspian walk happened in spring–summer **2023**, ending on the Kazakh Mangystau coast. The 2023 swim then fell through. They flew back in Aug 2024 ("After 6hrs we clear immigration") and started swimming from the point where they had stopped. Maxwell's "2024" may refer to the combined expedition. **Unresolved without the video description or a dated post.**
- Geographic tension: Maxwell's endpoint is "near the Turkmen border", while the swim is reported as starting "near Aktau" (about 200 km north of that border). Possibly the coast walk continued north to the Aktau area, or "near Aktau" is loose. [INF]
- Path: from Bukhara the walk would cross Navoiy/Karakalpakstan (Uchquduq or Nukus area?) and the Ustyurt into Mangystau via Beyneu. **No towns are sourced.** [INF]
- No Uzbek media (Kun.uz, Gazeta.uz, Upl.uz) coverage of the 2023/24 walk was found. Upl.uz covers only the 2026 finish.

## 8. Caspian Sea swim, 16 Aug–17 Sep 2024

**Before the swim**
- 16 Jul 2024: BBC (via AZERTAC/1news.az) announces a mid-August start. The Azerbaijani government is helping fund it ("stepped in when his expedition was short of funding"). He says "Failure here would be crippling" and that he is "definitely not a swimmer". — https://azertag.az/en/xeber/around_the_world_walker_set_to_swim_caspian_sea-3099250 ; https://1news.az/news/20240716122217135-Krugosvetnyi-puteshestvennik-nameren-pereplyt-Kaspii-iz-Kazakhstana-v-Azerbaidzhan
- 24 Jul 2024: in Baku, "We got to meet the ship and crew today that will support us." — OnTheGoSolo
- 8 Aug 2024: Times of Central Asia previews ~300 km Aktau→Baku, ~10 km/day, about a month, billed as the first-ever swim across the Caspian. — https://timesca.com/caspian-sea-swim-by-travelers-carl-bushby-and-angela-maxwell/
- 10 Aug 2024: the four-person team is on the east shore. Bushby expects "25–30 days" at 10–15 km/day. — OnTheGoSolo
- 15 Aug 2024 (Tengrinews/InAktau): start set for 16 Aug from Aktau. — https://en.tengrinews.kz/people/an-american-a-briton-and-azerbaijanis-decided-to-swim-across-263827
  - The team trained intensively in Aktau.
  - Support was entirely Azerbaijani: **one warship plus two regular vessels**.
  - Wetsuits ("diving equipment") because the sea is "often stormy and cold".
  - Routine: ~5 km before lunch, rest, ~5 km after (Boborikina).
  - Ages: Rustamov 18, Boborikina 16.

**Support (official site, snippet):** two escort vessels, "a local support boat from the Kazakh port of Aktau and an **Azerbaijani Coast Guard ship**". [CONFLICT with Tengrinews' "one warship and two vessels"; the coast-guard ship is probably the "warship".]

**During the swim**
- 16 Aug: "officially underway" after 6 hours of immigration clearance.
- 5 Sep: "approximately 75% of the way across"; arrival estimated around 12 Sep. It actually took 5 more days, implying weather days. [INF]
- Method:
  - Two 3-hour sessions a day (morning and afternoon), sleeping aboard and restarting at the logged exit point.
  - Bushby used a flotation vest and wetsuit ("floatimming", AP), and in training backstroke with fins.
  - On board they played **dominoes**. Sometimes they "just held on and tried to keep dinner down as their craft rocked violently".
  - Sources: ExplorersWeb 19 Oct 2024; Wikipedia; AP
- Weather [CONFLICT]:
  - ExplorersWeb: "dangerously rough seas and high winds"; 32 days at sea, 27 of them swimming days. The 5 non-swimming days were presumably lost to weather. [INF]
  - The official site (snippet): "battling waves, nausea and currents"; "unpredictable currents".
  - Bushby at the finish (AZERTAC): "We were lucky with the weather… nothing prevented us from swimming."
- **Jellyfish:** **no source found** mentions jellyfish. (Comb jellies, *Mnemiopsis*, are common in the Caspian, but nothing ties them to this swim.)
- **Injuries:** none reported in any source. Bushby said at the finish "I feel very well". Seasickness and nausea are the only physical complaints mentioned.
- No source gives a day-by-day log (daily km or stop positions). Bushby's Facebook and Instagram posts were not retrievable.

**Finish, 17 Sep 2024**
- Bushby's stats: "Task Complete: 18:30 – 17/9/2024… Distance: 275.5km… Hours swam: ~132hrs 21min… Days: 31". — OnTheGoSolo
- Landing place: the beach of the **Sea Breeze resort, Nardaran** (Absheron, ~30 min north of Baku), per Sea Breeze's own news post of 17 Sep 2024 (search snippet; page 403). It said "288 km border to border over 32 days". Habertürk/AA: a beach at a "holiday village" on the Azerbaijani coast. — https://seabreeze.az/en/news/famous-adventurers-swim-across-the-caspian-sea-to-sea-breeze ; https://www.haberturk.com/ingiliz-seyyah-hazar-denizi-ni-yuzerek-azerbaycan-a-gecti-3720832/3
- Welcome: Deputy Minister of Youth and Sports **Mariana Vasilyeva**, the Azerbaijan Swimming Federation, athletes and local people.
  - Passports were stamped with Azerbaijani entry on the beach.
  - Organisers: the Ministry of Youth and Sports and "Baku City Circuit" (AZERTAC: "Baku City Ring").
  - Bushby: "It was hard but we adapted and I think we did quite well in the end." He planned to "go home and return in a few weeks" to walk Azerbaijan, then Georgia.
  - Sources: AZERTAC 17 Sep 2024, https://azertag.az/ru/xeber/vsemirno_izvestnyi_puteshestvennik_nam_povezlo_s_pogodoi-3184406 ; Habertürk/AA 18 Sep 2024
- The official site (snippet) describes "a hero's welcome, backed by the Azerbaijani government".
- Figures conflict:
  - Distance: 275.5 km (Bushby's GPS) vs 288 km (standard) vs ~300 km (pre-swim).
  - Days: 31 (Bushby) vs 32 (ExplorersWeb, Sea Breeze) vs 41 (AZERTAC, wrong).
- After the swim:
  - Maxwell's site says the four "became the first people to swim across the Caspian Sea". No record body's confirmation was found.
  - Maxwell partnered with the Blue Marine Foundation.
  - Bushby continued alone afterwards; Maxwell did not continue with him.

## 9. Azerbaijan: Baku → Georgian border (Nov–Dec 2024)

- Habertürk/AA (18 Sep 2024) says he was going home and back "in a few weeks". [INF: the late Sep–early Nov gap is that break, likely in Mexico or the UK. Unsourced.]
- 9 Nov 2024 Facebook: "Leaving Baku and headed west. The last section of a 25 year life on the road begins". About 3,000 mi (5,000 km) remained via Türkiye and Bulgaria. — OnTheGoSolo
- The official site gives **10 Nov 2024**: "walked out of Baku and turned for the Bosphorus". It says Istanbul was reached 25 Apr 2025, "five months and more than two thousand kilometres later — across Azerbaijan, Georgia, and… Turkey". — snippet, https://karlbushby.world/story
- Route through Azerbaijan (Shamakhi/Ganja/Gazakh) and the border post (Red Bridge, or Lagodekhi from Balakan) are **not sourced**. [INF: Ganja–Gazakh–Red Bridge is the main road west]
- Georgia entry date: **not sourced**. ~450 km at ~25–30 km/day from 10 Nov gives **late Nov–early Dec 2024**. [INF]
- 30 Jan 2025: in Batumi after "a long month walking along busy highways" in rain and ice. — earlier notes
- Azerbaijani welcome in Nov 2024: no Azerbaijani media coverage was found of his departure from Baku or the walk west (AZERTAC, Report, Trend, APA all searched). The official welcome documented is the 17 Sep landing.
