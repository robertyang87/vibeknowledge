## 1. Headline new findings

1. **Answer to the rule-compliance question (the main gap in the earlier notes).** Keith Bushby, Karl's father, told The Hull Story:
   - "When he was arrested in Panama they took him forward in a helicopter and put him in jail."
   - "When they let him out he had to go back to where he was picked up so he could walk the distance again."
   - "He hasn't missed one yard out."
   - Source: [The Hull Story, Simon Bristow, 3 Mar 2025](https://www.thehullstory.com/allarticles/karl-bushby-long-walk-home).
   - The same article states the general rule: if he has to leave the route (visa or arrest), he marks the spot reached and returns there to continue.
   - El Tiempo's 8 Aug 2025 article is a Spanish re-quote of this interview: "Cuando lo soltaron, tuvo que regresar al lugar donde lo recogieron para poder caminar la distancia de nuevo" ([El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/cultura/gente/el-hombre-que-quiere-caminar-el-planeta-entero-y-viene-recorriendolo-desde-3479564)). The earlier notes cited only its first sentence.
   - "Taken *forward*" means the helicopter carried him beyond the arrest point, toward the jail.
   - INFERENCE: he restarted at Boca de Cupe, the arrest point, and walked from there toward the Pan-American Highway. No source names the restart town or date.
2. **Riosucio, Chocó was a key waypoint, and he called it one of the best days of the whole walk.**
   - When asked for his best day, Bushby answered: "The day I reached Riosucio on the Atrato River and had managed to avoid been gunned down by Colombian guerrillas" ([The Longest Way Home interview, posted 24 May 2010](https://www.thelongestwayhome.com/blog/great-modern-travelers/interview-with-karl-bushby/)).
   - [site-snippet] He found "brief sanctuary with soldiers in besieged Riosucio before pushing on through the jungle to Paya."
   - [site-snippet] Another passage says he moved "through dense canopy toward Riosucio — a town under siege — under cover of darkness, evading patrols over two gruelling nights" ([karlbushby.world](https://karlbushby.world/story), via search extract).
3. **New Cañasgordas detail (19 Jan 2001) [site-snippet].**
   - At the bridge over the **Río Sucio**, armed men tried to trap him and demanded his papers. He stayed calm and handed them over.
   - The entry calls Cañasgordas the edge of the area he had marked as a "hot spot".
   - The same entry (or the Dabeiba one, see §5) says: "last time there was a two-day battle; they mortared the town with bombs made from gas cylinders", and "right now there's a strong military presence."
4. **Dabeiba (Sun 21 Jan 2001) [site-snippet].**
   - He arrived at 15:30.
   - The previous night's hosts had warned that the next stretch would be very dangerous.
   - The town had been overrun three times by FARC. One attack was a two-day battle with gas-cylinder mortars, which ended when helicopter gunships arrived.
5. **Bajirá (Sun 28 Jan 2001) [site-snippet]**, a new detail beyond the signed memo already noted.
   - "The officer and his sergeant try one last time to change my mind, then desist."
   - The lieutenant recorded Bushby's decision **on a dictaphone** "so that they won't suffer comebacks when I turn up dead."
   - Conflict: the earlier notes say it was a written memo that both signed. Both may be true: memo and recording.
6. **Flotation method on the Atrato.** He was "stuffing empty soda bottles into his vest for buoyancy, and camouflaging himself under a mat of branches" over four days ([Men's Journal 2013](https://www.mensjournal.com/travel/karl-bushby-is-walking-around-the-world-no-really-20130514), via search extract; the direct fetch returned HTTP 403).
7. **Intelligence gathering before the jungle.** He had stayed at Colombian army and paramilitary radio bases and collected information "he shouldn't have had". He later drew it on a map for the senior Panamanian intelligence officer, which won his release ([Outdoor Magazyn, Julia Klimek, 25 Sep 2026](https://outdoormagazyn.pl/karl-bushby-i-przesmyk-darien-najtrudniejsza-przeprawa-goliath-expedition/)).
   - The same piece says that during the first, disguised stage he avoided towns in FARC-controlled areas and stayed in the jungle.
   - Boca de Cupe is called "the first Latino settlement" on his route; before it there were only Kuna villages.
   - Panamanian police were fighting FARC units that used the Panamanian side to escape the Colombian army and to rob villages.
8. **FARC "feet away".** Keith says FARC "could have killed him if they found him and at one point were just feet away" (paraphrased by [The Hull Story, 3 Mar 2025](https://www.thehullstory.com/allarticles/karl-bushby-long-walk-home)). Keith called the crossing "basically an escape and evasion exercise".
9. **Catalina ("Catty") details.**
   - *Giant Steps* pp. 125–130 cover meeting Catalina Estrada in Medellín. Wikipedia cites them as `{{rp|125–130}}` ([wikitext](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Karl_Bushby&action=raw)).
   - The Hull Story says he visited her when he returned to Colombia for visas, and that she tried for a time to join him on the walk ([link](https://www.thehullstory.com/allarticles/karl-bushby-long-walk-home)).
   - Backpacker: "walking the streets of Medellin with Estrada wrapped in his arms". Estrada, phoned in Madrid, said: "Karl is the big love of my life… I cried for seven years; I was so happy for seven years" ([Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/trips/the-long-way-home/?scope=anon)).
   - AP 2026: "He spent seven years with a Colombian woman" ([AP via KRMG](https://krmg.com/2026/10/31/in-28-years-of-walking-karl-bushby-has-overcome-jungle-desert-and-red-tape-now-hes-nearly-home/)).
   - In 2010 Bushby said "The girl I left behind in Colombia, now nine years ago…", which dates his leaving Medellín to early 2001. The relationship ended about two years before 2010 (The Longest Way Home).
   - Duration conflict: seven years (AP, Backpacker, roughly 2001–2008) versus "officially separated 2011" (La Razón, in the earlier notes).
10. **Medellín → Panama City took "two months" in total, and the jungle was described as "200 miles"** ([Intrepid Global Citizen](https://intrepidglobalcitizen.com/walking-the-world-for-30-years-karl-bushby-and-angela-maxwell/)). The miles figure conflicts with the site's "two hundred kilometres" and Onthegosolo's 90 miles.
   - INFERENCE: left Medellín about mid-Jan 2001 and reached Panama City about mid/late Mar 2001. That is roughly two months *including* the 18 days in jail, which fits. The earlier notes said "two months" probably included Medellín planning time; this source points instead to departure → Panama City.
11. **El Colombiano (2026):** "almas caritativas" gave him shelter and food when supplies ran out. In Panama he had reached canal territory by routes the authorities consider "pasos vetados", and he was jailed "por problemas de visado" ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/internacional/karl-bushby-regresa-casa-asi-recorrio-todo-el-mundo-caminando-LH43119763)).

## 3. Handling FARC and paramilitary danger: new specifics

- **Documented checkpoints of intent.** At Bajirá the army recorded his decision (memo plus dictaphone) so it would not be blamed.
- **Bridge stop at Cañasgordas** (§1.3): he complied calmly and handed over his papers. Paramilitaries stopped him without serious trouble (earlier notes).
- **Night movement** for two nights on the approach to Riosucio. He avoided towns in FARC areas.
- **Using the army.** He took brief sanctuary with soldiers in Riosucio and earlier stayed at army and paramilitary radio bases.
- **Camouflaged river travel:** bottles for buoyancy, branch mat, drifting near the bank as boats passed close.

## 5. Conflicts and corrections

- **"Released under police escort after British Embassy negotiations" is a conflation.** A search summary attributed this to Panama. In the actual Wikipedia text it belongs to **Xinjiang, China, 2018**: "following intervention by the British Embassy he was allowed to continue under restrictions and police escort" (wikitext). The seg1 note's "strict escort" site extract may be the same mix-up. Treat a Panama escort as **unverified**.
- **Abramovich/Prescott diplomacy** again showed up in a search extract tied to the La Palma entry. This is the 2006–08 Russia story. Discount it for Panama, as before.
- **Gas-cylinder mortar battle:** search extracts put it in the Cañasgordas entry once and the Dabeiba entry once. The Dabeiba attribution, with "overrun three times" and gunships, looks more specific. **Unresolved.**
- **La Honda (3 Feb) vs Riosucio order.** The site snippet says Riosucio came "before pushing on through the jungle to Paya". The river drift took four days.
  - INFERENCE: he reached Riosucio about 30 Jan – 1 Feb and then drifted *down* the Atrato (which flows north) with La Honda on 3 Feb, before striking west/north-west into the jungle toward Paya.
  - Alternative: he drifted first and reached Riosucio after. I could not place "La Honda" on a map from the sources.
- **Jungle length:** 200 miles (Intrepid) vs 200 km (site) vs 90 miles (Onthegosolo) vs 320 km in total (ExplorersWeb).
- **Catalina relationship length:** about 7 years (AP, Backpacker) vs separated in 2011 (La Razón).
- **Restricted area vs visa** as the reason for jail: both appear, often together (El Colombiano: visa; Colombia One and AP: restricted zone).
